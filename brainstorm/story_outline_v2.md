## 🌍 World & Setting

> **Status: PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** Core mechanics (gateway mechanic, two planes, gateway-mark effect on mortals) are in `wiki/concepts/`. Atmosphere and tone remain working material.

### The World at a Glance

- The world is undergoing significant, poorly-understood change
  - Significant decline from prior extravagance due to the decline of the gods
  - Less technological decline, more philosophical and moral collapse
    - The world is becoming smaller, more insular, more violent
    - People seek short-term and personal gains as the world grows more dangerous
- There are two planes: the divine plane and the mortal plane
  - The mortal plane is one world (unknown if there are more)
  - The world is recognizable: animals, plants, agrarian and ranching societies, standard beasts of burden
    - Some rare mythical beasts exist, mostly driven to extinction or hiding
    - Vegetarianism is considered more "noble" — that norm is eroding as ideals erode
  - The divine plane is mysterious and increasingly unstable
    - Gods lose memory and power crossing into the mortal world — long understood as simply the *cost* of transition
    - **The true mechanism:** mortal-built imitation gateways bleed divine energy *back* through the membrane in reverse — a slow, generational drain that nobody understands is happening
    - Gods can be aged or ageless; the logic of this is not understood
    - Some lose more than others in the crossing — now understood to correlate with proximity to imitation gateways at the site of their emergence

> **Status: RESOLVED.** See `wiki/concepts/gateway_mechanic.md` and `DECISIONS.md` (entries 2026-05-01 through 2026-05-10). The full mechanic, imitation gateways, mortal effects, and the irony at the center are all canon-committed there.

### The Gateway Mechanic *(Core World Rule)*

- Gateways were originally one-directional: thinnings of the membrane for divine passage *into* the mortal world
- Over generations, mortals began building imitation gateways at holy emergence sites — reverent mimicry, not malice
- These imitation gateways operate in reverse: small, individually negligible bleeds of divine energy *back* into the divine plane
- Collectively, across centuries, they are catastrophic
  - The divine plane is being slowly emptied and destabilized
  - Gods decline not from age or disease but from this reverse-drain — madness, loss of power, disappearance into wildness
  - The last god's emergence is not a herald's arrival — it is an *expulsion*, the divine plane pushing out its final inhabitant as it collapses
- **What remains in the divine plane once the last god crosses over is no longer divine — it is something else entirely**
- Mortals who live near imitation gateways are also affected, on a smaller scale
  - They experience a subtle, chronic drain — a narrowing of perception, a loss of the capacity for idealism
  - This is not understood as a symptom; it is simply how those people *are*

### Rules of This World

- Social structures orient around whichever deities have appeared nearby — even anti-deity societies are defined in opposition to them
- Many societies have a single patron deity; a few have cooperative groups of deities with aligned domains
- The "competitions" between gods are largely about amassing signs of influence: the beauty of their lands, the accomplishments of their peoples
- Powers vary enormously in scope — some gods are physically powerful, some are location-bound, some are wise beyond measure
  - Powers interact strangely in proximity to other gods — a source of both cooperation and unease
  - Powers are generally used for the benefit of others, but as gods decline, this norm breaks down
- When a god dies, what happens is not fully known — this is a source of significant dread and speculation

> **Status: WORKING MATERIAL.** Tone guidance for all agents. Not resolved into a discrete wiki entry; it informs `STYLE_AND_VOICE.md` and `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §3.8.

### Atmosphere & Tone

- Sad mystery — the dominant feeling is loss without explanation
- People respond in the full range: despair, opportunism, hiding, surviving, revolting
  - Some humans are killing peaceful gods out of preemptive fear
  - Some gods are becoming paranoid that their people will turn on them — correctly
- There is a pervasive grief for a world that used to exist and is not coming back
- The irony underneath everything: the reverence that built imitation gateways — an act of love and devotion — is what destroyed the divine

## 🎭 Characters

> **Status: PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** The Protagonist and The Dying Deity have full wiki entries (`wiki/characters/the_protagonist.md`, `wiki/characters/the_dying_deity.md`). The Last God has a stub entry (`wiki/characters/`). Supporting characters (the maddened god, the gateway-builder, the anti-deity figure, the god who recognized the mechanism) remain the TBD table below — working material.

### The Mortal *(Protagonist — name TBD, she/her)*

- **Role:** Main POV. Drives the plot forward, given a quest she doesn't want or understand. Must discover truths about the world and truths about herself.
- **Want (surface):** To kill the god she encounters at the tree — a specific, charged desire rooted in personal history
- **Want (middle):** To fulfill the quest despite herself — she doesn't know why she's pressing so hard to make it
- **Need (deep):** To understand that her perception of the world — which she experiences as clarity — is itself a wound, and that wound is also what makes her uniquely capable of seeing the truth at the end
- **The Secret:** She is gateway-marked. Born near or exposed to an imitation gateway, she has been slowly drained her whole life. Her inability to see ideals or beauty in the world is not personality — it is symptom. She is a living record of what the gateways do.
- **Why She Is Chosen:** The dying deity sees the mark on her and partially misreads it — interpreting her stripped-down perception as useful *clarity* rather than understanding it as *evidence*. The choice is right. The reasoning is incomplete.
- **Gender & the World:** She is a woman navigating a world in accelerating collapse. The costs of that collapse fall unevenly, and she has borne more than her share. Her gritty, unillusioned worldview has been confirmed over and over by circumstance — which makes it nearly impossible for her to recognize that the drain is also *happening to her*, not just *reflecting* the world accurately.
- **Voice / Feel:** Gritty, unillusioned, occasionally bitter. Her narration is the lens through which the world's ugliness is most visible.
- **Arc:** She begins not believing in the quest, completes it out of a compulsion she can't name, and at the moment of the last god's emergence feels the drain *consciously for the first time* — recognizing in her body what the dying deity's investigation couldn't prove.

### The Dying Deity *(Secondary POV — name TBD, he/him)*

- **Role:** Major character, deity. Appears at the opening scene, entrusts the protagonist with the quest, dies — but lives in flashback chapters that run parallel to her journey.
- **Want (surface):** To understand why the gods are declining before he dies. To ensure the last god is properly met.
- **Need (deep):** To be right — and to accept that being *almost* right, and trusting the right person anyway, is enough.
- **The Wound:** He sees the world as pure ideal. His perception is genuinely beautiful and genuinely blind. He investigates the decline with rigor and love, and arrives at the correct *relationship* (gateways are the mechanism) but the wrong *direction* (he believes the decline is causing the gateways to fray, not that the gateways are causing the decline).
- **The Tragedy:** The tragedy is not his death. It is that he completes his investigation, arrives at his conclusion with confidence, and is wrong in a way he cannot know. His trust in her is his most correct act — and he doesn't know exactly why it's correct.

### The Last God *(name TBD)*

- **Role:** Arrives at the climax. The first thing they say reframes everything.
- **Nature:** Not a herald. An expulsion. They did not choose to come — they were pushed out as the divine plane collapsed around them.
- **The Line:** Something to the effect of: *"I am not the last god. I am the last thing that was a god. What remains behind me is no longer that."*

## 🧵 Themes

> **Status: WORKING MATERIAL.** These are the questions the story is asking, not settled facts. See `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §4.1 for the four load-bearing thematic questions that are author-decision-only. This section remains live throughout all phases.

### Central Theme

- **Trust in the goodness of others — with incomplete information.** Paranoia and fear are rational responses to a collapsing world. But trust, even imperfect trust, even trust based on slightly wrong reasoning, is the only thing that threads the needle.

### Secondary Themes

- **Reverence as unintentional destruction.** The imitation gateways were built out of love. The catastrophe is not the result of malice but of devotion without understanding.
- **The veneer of civilization.** When the structures holding things together begin to fail, what lies beneath is not always barbarism — sometimes it is grief.
- **Perception as wound.** Both main characters are limited by how they see. The mortal sees too little beauty; the deity sees too much. Neither has the complete picture.
- **What do we do when things go wrong in ways we cannot understand or solve?**

### Questions the Story Asks *(without necessarily answering)*

- What is owed between gods and the people who depend on them?
- How much should we trust the origin stories and sacred structures we inherit?
- Can we know our own histories — or only the myths we've made of them?
- Is the loss of the divine plane a tragedy, a correction, or something the story refuses to categorize?

## 📖 Story Structure

> **Status: WORKING MATERIAL.** Two-thread architecture, opening, core tension — all planned but not yet drafted. Phase 3 begins Scene 1 (the tree). This section is the spine the main narrative will be built on.

### Narrative Architecture

Two interwoven threads with different temporal registers:

**Thread A — The Mortal's Journey (present tense of the story)**
The mortal, compelled by the quest, travels toward the appointed place and time. Along the way she encounters the declining world — wild gods, collapsing societies, imitation gateways, the human consequences of the drain. The journey is also an internal one: fragments of her past surface, and the reader begins to understand the gateway-mark before the mortal does.

**Thread B — The Deity's Investigation (recent past, told in flashbacks)**
The dying deity investigates the cause of the divine decline. Each flashback is a chapter of his inquiry — he moves through the world differently than the mortal does, seeing its luminosity even as he documents its decay. The investigation builds toward a conclusion. The conclusion is almost right.

**The convergence:** The deity's final flashback — his confident, almost-right conclusion — lands just before the mortal arrives at the appointed place. The reader holds both the deity's frame and the mortal's perception simultaneously as the last god emerges.

### The Opening

- A tree. Unremarkable. The dying deity and the protagonist meet here.
- She wants to kill him. The defusal: her hatred is a load-bearing structure, and the reality of what she finds — a man, a god, dying, and somehow *undefeated* — offers her hatred nothing to grip.
- He speaks as if the conversation he is having is a completely different one than the one she came to have.
- The quest is given: be at a certain place at a certain time. Meet who emerges.
- **The scene will be revisited from his POV in a flashback** — the two accounts of the same moment will be almost irreconcilable. The gap between them is where the theme lives.

### The Core Tension

- The mortal pressing toward the appointed place against her own nature — she doesn't trust, doesn't believe, doesn't hope, and yet she goes
- The deity's flashbacks moving toward a truth he can almost touch
- The reader slowly understanding what neither character fully does

## 🎬 Scenes & Moments

> **Status: WORKING MATERIAL — Phase 3+.** The five spine scenes are planned; none are drafted yet. Scene 1 (the tree) is the Phase 3 target. See `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §5 for phase order.

### Scene 1 — The Tree
The opening. The dying deity and the mortal, at an unremarkable tree. The mortal's desire to kill is present and somehow not acted on. The quest is laid down not as a command but as a statement of fact: *you will be there. I know you will.* The mortal leaves. The compulsion is already inside them like a splinter.

### Scene 2 — The Deity Notices the Imitation Gateways
A flashback. The deity walks through an ancient emergence site, now heavily built over with mortal imitations. They feel something wrong — a wrongness they can't name. This is the moment they were closest to the truth.

### Scene 3 — The Maddened God *(suggested)*
Mid-journey. The mortal encounters a god deep in decline — not yet gone, but no longer fully coherent. The god is near an imitation gateway. The mortal notices that they feel *strange* near it — a familiar strangeness. This is the reader's first real signal of the mortal's mark.

### Scene 4 — The Deity's Conclusion
The final flashback. The deity assembles their investigation and arrives at their answer. They believe the cause flows from divine decline outward. They are wrong about the direction. The conclusion is delivered with quiet confidence. It is heartbreaking.

### Scene 5 — The Last God Emerges
The appointed place. The mortal arrives. The emergence is unlike any recorded — the gate tears rather than opens. The last god comes through. They say the line. The mortal feels the drain — consciously, for the first time — and understands something they cannot put into words. They act.

## 🔮 Symbols, Motifs & Recurring Images

> **Status: WORKING MATERIAL.** Craft-level guidance; not resolved into wiki entries. Use as reference when drafting scenes to check for motif consistency.

- **Gateways — divine and imitation.** What looks like reverence becomes extraction.
- **Blindness and sight.** Both POV characters perceive the world incompletely — but differently.
- **The tree.** An unremarkable thing at the center of everything.
- **Memory and loss.** The gods lose memory crossing over. Mortals lose something subtler and slower near the gateways.
- **Animals behaving strangely near imitation gateways.** A quiet recurring detail.
- **Food and violence.** The eroding norm of vegetarianism as civilizational barometer.
