# Main Timeline

A reference for the temporal structure of *The Doors of Gods*. Tracks key events and their rough positions relative to each other, not absolute dates. The world does not use a calendar system that maps onto Earth years; temporal relationships are expressed as *before*, *during*, and *after* relative to anchor events.

The three structural layers of time in the story:

1. **Mythic past** — the world before significant decline; the era when imitation gateways spread
2. **Prologue era** — the specific emergence the prologue depicts; the witness girl's childhood
3. **Present era** — the fragment dossier events; the main story; the last god's emergence

---

## Layer 1: Mythic Past (undated)

These events are background — they are not dramatized in the story, but their effects are present in every scene.

**The age of emergence** — Gods crossed freely into the mortal world. Each crossing left a visible threshold — a door-shaped thinning of the membrane — and produced cultures, sacred sites, and eventually priestly traditions organized around the moment of crossing.

**The first imitation gateways** — Mortals who witnessed emergences began building architectural imitations at holy sites: acts of devotion, not malice. These constructions ran the gateway mechanic in reverse, creating a slow, negligible-per-site drain of divine energy back through the membrane.

**The accumulation problem** — Over centuries, the aggregate drain became catastrophic. Gods began declining — not from age or disease but from the reverse-drain. The connection between the imitation gateways and the decline was never made; the gateways were symptoms of reverence, not suspected causes. Priestly classes formed around the gateways and had every incentive not to understand what they were doing.

**The spreading decline** — Gods began going wild (retreating from their domains into the margins), declining into absence, or arriving from crossings already diminished. The world became smaller, more insular, more violent, as the invisible social substrate that gods provided frayed.

> *Temporal note:* The distance between the first imitation gateways and the present era is generational — centuries, not decades. The world the fragment dossier depicts is well into the late phase of this process. How many generations deep this goes is not yet settled in canon.

## Layer 2: Prologue Era

**Anchor event:** The unnamed emergence in the protagonist's home village — a god crossed into the mortal world on the upper slope above the village, witnessed by a child (the witness girl).

### What is established

- The witness girl is approximately 8 years old at the time of the prologue (*An Emergence*, Fragment 1)
- Pre-emergence effects were present for some duration before the crossing (animals restless, sky wrong, domestic rhythms disturbed)
- The god arrived alone, confused, diminished — wet-haired, looking at his hands; he had lost more in the crossing than a god should
- The witness girl was physically altered by proximity to the crossing: her handedness flipped (she crossed the slope right-handed; she came down left-handed)
- The village found words for the man on the slope; none of those words touched what actually changed
- Pa walked down from the high pasture four days later in the company of the man who had emerged — and with Margot (the goat who had first reached the crossing site)
- The village's mythology of the emergence formed around pa's descent with the stranger, not around the witness girl's account

### Temporal distance from the present era

- The witness girl is an *older woman* in the main story — she will appear or be referenced there
- A rough working estimate: **40–60 years before the present era** (the witness girl is perhaps 8 in the prologue; she is meaningfully old, but still living, in the main story)
- This is a **working estimate only** — author decision deferred. The exact gap affects how the village's mythology has had time to form and how much the witness girl remembers.

### What is not yet settled

- Whether the god in the prologue is the dying deity of the main story arriving early in his mortal life, or a different god entirely (see `DECISIONS.md` 2026-05-01 — identity held open)
- The witness girl's role in the main story (meaningfully present character vs. referenced figure)
- The exact interval between the prologue and the present era

## Layer 3: Present Era

The era in which the fragment dossier events occur and in which the main narrative is set.

All fragments in the dossier are set during the present era — the late-stage decline, when the effects are becoming undeniable but the mechanism is still unrecognized.

### Events with known relative positions

**Fragment 2: *The Vaelen at the Third Bell* (Vanaek)**
The accord-domain god of Vanaek is in active decline. The decline is measurable but not yet public knowledge. Relen's commercial coordination failures are a downstream effect. Sorin is documenting from inside the council structure.

**Fragment 3: *The Count* (Solaven)**
Tavoen is on their third year of observation at the Tavorael. The decline count: 4/67 failures (year 1), 13/53 (year 2), 35/41 (year 3) — the acceleration is severe. The witness-god still has residual presence but is clearly near collapse.

**Fragment 4: *The Wild God* (Eastern Wild Margins)**
The group from the sound-valley has already left their home settlement. The sound god whose domain produced the valley's particular resonance has sufficiently declined that its people were displaced (proximity-changes — permanent tinnitus — drove the departure). They traveled to the eastern margins seeking an ancient wild god that reportedly does not decline. The attempt failed: the wild god is too old and too diffuse to function as a patron.

**Fragment 5: *The City After* (The Frozen City)**
The volcanic event was approximately **two years before** the historian's visit. The preservation god entered stasis during the event; the incoming god (natural-processes-unfolding domain) redirected the main flow and died. The preservation god's stasis domain continues operating ambient — the frozen district is held, two years later, in an unchanged moment.

### Temporal relationship between fragments

The fragments are likely **roughly contemporary** with each other — within a few decades at most. They are not causally connected (no character from one fragment appears in another) but they depict the same historical moment: the late phase of a decline that has been building for generations.

The fragments are also **roughly contemporary with the main narrative** — the events the protagonist and the dying deity move through are happening in the same world these fragments depict, under the same general conditions.

> *Note for continuity:* If the main narrative establishes that certain places appear as locations (e.g. Vanaek, Solaven, the Frozen City are visited), the fragments' timeline positions will need to be checked against the narrative's internal calendar.

## Layer 3b: The Dying Deity's Flashback Thread

The dying deity's chapters are set in **his past** — he has lived in the mortal world for some duration before the main story begins. His flashbacks are not yet drafted, but they will cover:

- His early mortal years (arrival, adjustment, beginning to notice the decline)
- His investigation — the arc of his near-correct conclusion
- Key relationships and events that form his understanding before the tree

### What is established

- He arrived from the divine plane already diminished — the prologue may or may not depict this arrival (see `DECISIONS.md` on the identity question)
- He has been in the mortal world long enough to have built an incomplete but near-correct theory about the gateway mechanic
- His mortal life ends at the tree — Scene 1 of the main narrative

> *Note:* The dying deity's flashback timeline will need a separate entry or section expansion once Phase 3 drafting begins.

## The Last God

The last god's emergence — the climax of the main narrative — marks the effective end of the divine plane. His crossing is an expulsion, not an arrival: the plane pushing out its final inhabitant as it collapses behind him.

**Temporal position:** Future relative to all existing fragments; the culmination of the main narrative.

**What remains after:** Not yet settled in canon. What fills the space left by the divine plane is one of the story's load-bearing unanswered questions.
