# Decline

The process by which a god's presence, domain, and capacity diminish over time. The root cause is the mortal-built imitation gateway network — structures built across the settled world as acts of devotion to connect the mortal and divine planes, which in practice drain divine energy back in reverse, slowly collapsing the divine plane. No mortal (and very few gods) understands this mechanism. Gods experience decline as a loss of capacity and clarity; mortals experience it as the failure of what the god's presence had always made possible.

## The mechanism (canonical; not yet diegetically understood)

The imitation gateways were built by mortals out of genuine devotion — as places of sacred approach, as architectural expressions of relationship to the divine. They appear to function: they feel like thresholds, ritual at them produces a sense of nearness to the divine, priestly classes have organized entire liturgical traditions around them. What they actually do is run the gateway mechanic in reverse. A god's emergence creates a threshold that transfers the god from the divine plane to the mortal plane; an imitation gateway creates a threshold that transfers divine energy from the divine plane to nowhere. The drain is gradual, cumulative, and invisible.

No character in the story, as of the fragment dossier phase, understands this mechanism completely. The readers of decline observe the symptoms; the anti-deity movements name the harm without the cause; the priestly classes suppress diagnosis. The dying deity is moving toward a partial understanding.

## What decline looks like from outside

Decline does not proceed uniformly. The visible signs depend on the god's domain, the community's structure, and how far the decline has progressed.

**Early decline** is almost entirely invisible. The god's ambient effects are slightly diminished; the domain's quality is marginally reduced. People who have never known the god at full capacity cannot detect this. Even attentive observers may only note a vague sense that something is subtly different, less vivid, slightly off — without being able to name what they're comparing it to.

**Middle decline** produces the characteristic pattern the fragments document: domain-specific failures that manifest as social or environmental texture before anyone has language for them. The accord domain produces social mis-rhythms. The witness domain produces rituals that complete without their characteristic exchange. The sound domain produces tinnitus. These signs are legible to trained observers (readers of decline) and felt but unnameable by the community.

**Late decline** is usually characterized by the ritual persisting in form while the substance is entirely gone; the priestly class's concealment becoming difficult to maintain; community fragmentation; and increasingly desperate responses. In patron-model societies this is often when community members attempt to resolve the absence by intensifying ritual, making pilgrimages, or — as in Fragment 4 — seeking a different god.

**Terminal decline / the god's end** is not yet documented in the fragment dossier. What happens to a god at the complete end of decline — whether it dies, departs, becomes something else, or simply thins away — is held open.

## What decline looks like from inside (the god's experience)

Not yet established in canon for the dossier fragments. The main narrative will develop the dying deity's experience of his own decline as one of the two central POVs. Current brainstorm notes suggest: increasing difficulty perceiving the mortal world clearly; a narrowing of domain; a tendency toward perception that is either too-vivid (hyper-luminous, missing detail) or too-dim. The divine perception problem — that the dying deity sees too much beauty — is both a symptom of decline and the specific shape of his misunderstanding of the protagonist.

## Domain-specific signatures

Each god's decline produces proximity-marks and environmental effects shaped by the nature of its domain. The pattern across the fragments:

| God / Domain | Decline signature | Fragment |
|---|---|---|
| Accord god (Vanaek) | Social mis-rhythms; coordination failures; conversations missing beats | Fragment 2 |
| Witness god (Solaven) | Ritual completing without exchange; participants feeling personal failure | Fragment 3 |
| Sound god (the valley) | Permanent tinnitus in proximity-bearers; broken intervals of the domain's former music | Fragment 4 |
| Preservation god (the city) | Stasis domain running past the god's waking; geological change from a single catastrophic event | Fragment 5 |

## Decline vs. departure vs. absence

These are distinct categories that communities often conflate:
- **Decline:** The god is still present but diminished. The domain is failing. The god may not know this.
- **Departure:** The god left — possibly voluntarily, possibly through loss of the capacity to remain. The community may not know which occurred.
- **Absence:** The god is gone. Whether through decline-to-end, departure, or something else is often unknown. Absent-god communities have adapted to this state.

## Notes for future scenes

The question of whether any mortal will understand the actual mechanism before the divine plane collapses entirely is one of the story's structural questions. The protagonist's journey toward that understanding — and the dying deity's parallel movement toward almost-understanding — is the main narrative's spine.
