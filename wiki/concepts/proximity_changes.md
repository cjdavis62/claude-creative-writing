# Proximity Changes

Permanent or long-lasting alterations to mortal bodies, senses, or cognition produced by proximity to divine presence. Proximity changes are domain-specific — the nature of the change reflects the nature of the god — and vary in intensity based on proximity and, in some cases, birth-location relative to the god's domain-hall. Both emergence and decline produce them, through different mechanisms.

## Key principles

**Domain-specificity.** Proximity changes are shaped by the god's domain, not by some generic divine property. The sound god's people bear tinnitus — the broken music of what the domain was. A god of witness might alter what its people remember. A preservation god's proximity-marks would involve states of continuation or stasis. Each god writes itself into its people in its own idiom.

**Permanence.** Most proximity changes are permanent once established. They do not diminish when the god leaves or declines. The tinnitus in the sound god's community persists after the god's decline; the prologue witness's handedness flip persists after the emergence is over. Proximity to another divine presence may suppress a proximity-mark (as in Fragment 4, where the almost-mythic wild god's presence temporarily silences the tinnitus), but the mark returns when proximity ends. Suppression is not reversal.

**Gradient by proximity and origin.** The intensity of proximity changes correlates with how near to the god's core presence the mortal was — physically and in terms of where they were born. Those born nearest the domain-hall carry the strongest marks. Those who arrived at the settlement later or lived farther from the hall carry lighter versions. The most severe marks are in those whose entire early development occurred within the full expression of the domain.

**Not necessarily visible.** Some proximity changes are sensory or cognitive rather than physical. The handedness flip in the prologue witness is physical and externally observable. The tinnitus is internal and auditory. The gateway-mark (the protagonist's drain from proximity to imitation gateways) may be cognitive — affecting perception in ways that are hard to distinguish from personality. Not all marks announce themselves.

## Established proximity changes (by fragment)

### Prologue — emergence of the god (domain unknown)

**The handedness flip:** The witness girl went up the slope right-handed and came down left-handed. A physical inversion produced by proximity to an emergence — consistent with the emergence's cosmological signature (the air folding, the light inverting, the world briefly turning inside out). The change is permanent. The witness does not name it; the prose plants it through action and names it plainly once in the ending without explanation.

*Note: the brainstorm establishes that gateway-marked mortals are drained in a distinct way. The handedness flip is from emergence proximity, not gateway proximity — the mechanisms may be different. See [[emergence]].*

### Fragment 4 — the sound god's decline

**Tinnitus:** The sound god's domain gave the valley a quality of resonance — a way voices fell, a depth to ordinary sound. Its decline produced the broken ghost of that gift: permanent ringing in all those who bore the proximity-mark, with intensity proportional to birth-proximity to the domain-hall. In at least one case (the woman born within sight of the hall), the ringing had developed into audible patterns — rhythms, intervals, the ghost of the god's former music. This is the domain's signature persisting as its own negative image.

**Tinnitus suppression near the almost-mythic wild god:** In Fragment 4, the sound god's proximity-marks were temporarily suppressed by proximity to the almost-mythic wild god in the eastern margins. The ringing stopped; it returned when the group began to withdraw. This confirms: (1) proximity to a sufficiently present divine being can quiet an existing mark; (2) the suppression is not permanent; (3) the return of the tinnitus on withdrawal confirms the permanence of the original mark.

### Fragment 5 — the preservation god's stasis event

The frozen district itself is a proximity change applied to a place rather than to people — the god's stasis domain written into the physical environment at enormous scale. The category of "proximity change" is typically applied to mortals; the frozen district extends the concept. The persistence of the stasis after the god entered its own stasis parallels the persistence of tinnitus after the sound god's decline: domains outlast the god's active consciousness.

### The protagonist — gateway proximity (established; not yet narratively rendered)

The protagonist bears a **gateway-mark** from proximity to imitation gateways — a drain on her perception and capacity that has been present since before she can remember, and which she cannot distinguish from her ordinary self. The brainstorm describes her narration as "unillusioned" — a narrowed perception that misses certain kinds of beauty but is also unusually clear about others. The dying deity reads her mark as unusual clarity rather than as evidence of drain; this misreading is one of the story's central ironic structures.

*The specific perceptual shape of the gateway-mark is not yet fully settled in canon.*

## The threshold-of-life principle and proximity

The very young and very old are more sensitive to divine presence generally (see [[emergence]]). This applies to proximity changes: those at the thresholds of life may receive stronger marks from the same proximity, or marks in different registers, than those in the middle of life. Fragment 3 shows a related phenomenon: the declining witness god's residual presence still responds to a twelve-year-old when it fails to respond to forty adults — not because the girl is special, but because she has not yet built the adult accommodation to the domain's failing texture.

## Open questions

- Whether the protagonist's gateway-mark is unique in kind (not just in degree) among proximity changes. The brainstorm suggests it may be — the gateway mechanic running in reverse may produce a fundamentally different alteration than direct god-proximity.
- Whether proximity changes can be reversed under any circumstances, or are strictly permanent.
- Whether god-adjacent figures (clergy, long-term attendants) accumulate proximity changes differently from ordinary community members.
- Whether the proximity changes in a community persist as cultural or behavioral traits after the original mark-bearers are gone — does the tinnitus die with its generation, or does it leave traces in how the community's descendants hear?
