# The Gateway Mechanic

The central mechanism of the world. The cause of divine decline. The tragedy underneath everything. It is not yet understood by any mortal character in the story, and only partially approached by the dying deity.

## The original gateway

When a god emerges into the mortal world, the crossing creates a visible threshold — a door-shaped thinning of the membrane between planes, through which the god passes and through which a glimpse of elsewhere can briefly be seen. The threshold closes after the god is through. It does not remain open; it is not a passage for return.

This gateway operates in one direction: divine plane to mortal plane. The god crosses. Something is lost in the crossing — memory, power — but the god arrives. The threshold closes. The membrane heals.

This is the original mechanic. It is the one thing.

## The imitation gateway

Mortals who witnessed emergences — or who lived near emergence sites, or who built cultures around the idea of divine approach — began constructing imitation gateways at sacred sites. They were built out of love. They were built as acts of devotion: architectural expressions of relationship to the divine, places of sacred approach, the mortal world's way of saying *we are ready for you here*. They feel, to those who use them, like thresholds. Ritual at them produces a sense of nearness to the divine. Priestly traditions have organized entire liturgical systems around them.

They run the gateway mechanic in reverse.

A true gateway transfers a god from the divine plane to the mortal plane. An imitation gateway creates a threshold that transfers divine energy from the divine plane to nowhere — a slow, small bleed in the wrong direction. Each individual imitation gateway is negligible. Collectively, across centuries and across thousands of sacred sites on every road the mortal world has built toward divinity, they are catastrophic.

## The effect on the divine plane

The divine plane is being slowly emptied and destabilized. Gods decline not from age or disease but from this reverse-drain — loss of power, loss of clarity, eventual loss of coherent selfhood (what the brainstorm calls madness). The gods who go wild, the gods who decline into absence, the gods who arrive in the mortal world having lost more than they should have — these are downstream effects of the drain.

The last god's emergence is not a herald's arrival. It is an expulsion: the divine plane pushing out its final inhabitant as it collapses around him. What remains behind him in the divine plane after that crossing is no longer divine — it is something else entirely.

## The effect on mortals

Mortals who live near imitation gateways are also affected, on a smaller scale. They experience a chronic, subtle drain — a narrowing of perception, a loss of the capacity for idealism, a world that looks more stripped and less full than it might. This is not understood as a symptom. It is simply how those people are. It is confirmed over and over by the world they actually live in — a world that is, genuinely, becoming worse — which makes it nearly impossible to distinguish *what the drain has done* from *what the world has shown them*.

The protagonist is the canonical example: gateway-marked from early life, she cannot distinguish her perception from her personality. The dying deity reads her mark as clarity. She experiences it as the world simply being what it is.

## What is not understood

No mortal character in the story (as of the fragment dossier phase) understands the gateway mechanism. The range of existing understanding:

- **Priestly classes:** Know that the divine is connected to the sacred sites; do not know that the sites are the problem.
- **Readers of decline:** Know that gods are declining; have varying and none-quite-correct theories about why. The closest theory — that the imitation gateways are implicated — is held by some, but the direction is usually misread (they think the decline is weakening the gateways, not that the gateways are causing the decline).
- **Anti-deity movements:** Know that divine presence has caused harm; believe the solution is removing divine presence. Would, if successful, eliminate the remaining divine maintenance without addressing the drain.
- **The dying deity:** Arrives at the correct relationship (gateways implicated) but inverts the cause and effect. His conclusion is the most nearly-correct in the world, and it is wrong in a specific and decisive way.
- **The protagonist:** Carries the evidence in her body. Does not know she is carrying it. At the climax, when she feels the drain consciously for the first time, she understands what the dying deity could not quite close on — from the inside, in the body, in the moment it matters.

## The irony at the center

The imitation gateways were built out of genuine devotion. The mortals who built them were not wrong to love the divine. They were not malicious. They were reverent, and their reverence found the only expression it had access to, and that expression was the mechanism of destruction. The catastrophe is not the result of malice but of love without understanding.

This is what the dying deity means when he investigates with love — he is trying to understand a catastrophe that was also caused by love. The theme of the novel is trust with incomplete information; the world's tragedy is devotion with incomplete information. They rhyme.

## Relationship to emergence

The visible gateway at an emergence — the upright threshold, the glimpse of elsewhere, the door that closes behind the god — is related to but distinct from the imitation gateway. Whether the imitation gateway is a successful replication of the emergence mechanism (running in reverse) or something that only resembles it while operating on different principles is not yet settled in canon. The visible similarity is what led mortals to build them; the functional difference is the catastrophe.

## Notes for the writer-agent

The gateway mechanic is the world's most important secret. It should never be stated directly in the prose before the moment it is discovered. It should be approachable — close readings of various scenes should make it feel, in retrospect, like it was always there to be seen — without being legible on first encounter. Every scene that involves an imitation gateway, a sacred site, a reader of decline's methodology, or the protagonist's perception is an opportunity to plant evidence. The evidence should feel, before the revelation, like texture.
