# Emergence

The event of a god crossing from the divine plane into the mortal world. Sudden, locationally specific, never announced. The god arrives incomplete (memory and power lost in transit, per the brainstorm) and disoriented; the witness has no precedent for what they are seeing.

## Pre-emergence effects (canon as of `prologue_emergence.md` v3)

The membrane between planes does not break suddenly without warning at the *physical* level — only at the level of human perception. For some duration before a god's actual arrival (likely hours, possibly longer; not yet specified), the membrane at the emergence site is straining, thinning, preparing to give. Mortals do not generally perceive this. Animals do.

The pre-emergence period produces:

- **Restlessness in animals across the surrounding area, not just the emergence site.** Domestic animals — dogs, chickens, cats — become unsettled, refuse normal behaviors, will not come when called or settle when fed. The village will read this as "a strange day" without naming what makes it strange.
- **Animals in close proximity to the impending site are *drawn* toward it.** The behavior is not understood by the animal but is acted on. A goat in a familiar pasture may simply leave, walking with a directness goats do not normally have, traveling further than they would normally travel, ignoring grass they would normally eat. They go to the place where the membrane will give.
- **At the site, animals that have arrived first stand still, attentive to a point in the air where there is nothing yet.** They have arrived early and they wait, intent. They are the first witnesses.

This means that in retrospect, the *first* witness of any emergence is almost always an animal — and the actual first human witness is whoever happened to follow the animal. The brainstorm's note that "the person who actually witnessed the emergence first is not always the person who ends up with the authority that derives from it" can be extended: the entity that witnessed it first is rarely human at all, and is never given any authority.

### Celestial signs

In addition to animal restlessness, the night sky shows pre-emergence wrongness for those attentive enough to read it:

- **Constellations may show wrong angles or extra stars.** A familiar constellation appears subtly off — one star too bright for its season, one star displaced, or stars showing in places between the constellations where stars are not normally visible. The wrongness is generally subtle enough that observers cannot be certain — only the most attentive sky-watchers will notice, and they will not be sure.
- **The night sky as a whole feels too bright.** Too many stars; stars too sharp. The way a too-clear winter night feels even in seasons that are not winter.

These signs are read by sky-watchers and herders who track the night sky in the course of their work; they are missed by anyone not paying close attention. The wrongness is never "the stars rearrange themselves" — that would be too gross to miss. It is "something is subtly off" — the kind of detail a child knows in her bones but cannot defend if asked.

### Sensitivity at the thresholds of life

Animals are more sensitive to emergence than humans. The prologue extends this: humans at the *thresholds of life* — the very young and the very old — also tend to be more sensitive than those in the middle of life. Children sometimes witness emergences that the adults around them do not perceive at all; the very old (and the very old of other species, like an old goat) are similarly drawn or unsettled. The pairing of young and old around an emergence is itself a sign — though, like the others, it is rarely read as one until the mythology has had time to form.

This is one of the reasons emergences tend to be witnessed by figures who would not otherwise have been on the path: the child sent to fetch the goat, the elderly herder who happened to be walking near, the dying scholar in their last weeks. None of them stand for "neutral observation"; all of them are at the edges of human cognition where the membrane is more easily felt.

## Sensory signature (canon as of `prologue_emergence.md`)

The brainstorm establishes that emergences have physical signatures that vary by god — light, sound, displacement of air, sensation in the body of anyone nearby. The prologue commits the prose to a more specific shape:

- **The air "folds."** Briefly, the air around the emergence point behaves like cloth, then like water, then resumes being air — but the air it resumes being is subtly different from the air it was. This is the prose's preferred descriptor; finding the phrase across multiple POVs and accounts will be one of the running motifs.
- **Witnesses feel a physical signature** — cold on the arms, prickling in the skin — *before* visual recognition catches up. The body knows first.
- **A visible upright threshold appears in the air.** Roughly door-sized — about as wide as outstretched arms, as tall as a person and then taller. Color is hard to fix in language; the prologue's witness later describes it as "the color of looking at the sun through closed eyelids."
- **Through the threshold, a glimpse of somewhere not here.** A horizon that does not belong to the local geography. Light along it that may move the wrong way. The glimpse is brief — "the breath of a breath" — and closes once the god has crossed.
- **The threshold closes after the god is through.** It does not remain open; it is not a door for return.
- **The god arrives disoriented.** Hands held out from the body as if they had just set down weight; clothes immaculate even where the ground is dust; small unaccountable details (wet hair in the prologue) that vary by god. They look at the world, and at their own hands, as if seeing them for the first time. They may press a hand to their chest as though checking whether they are breathing.
- **Light does not behave correctly around them.** The shadow falls right; the ground near them carries a brightness that has no source.
- **Animals nearby become still.** Goats stop chewing; pines stop swaying; the wind does not come.
- **Their first speech is felt as well as heard.** The word travels into the witness's body, not only through the air. The word may be in no language the god can now remember, having been lost in transit.

## What is *not* committed yet

- Whether the visible threshold is the same phenomenon as the imitation gateways mortals have built at sacred sites, only operating in the original direction. Likely, but not stated in canon.
- Whether all witnesses see the threshold, or only some. The prologue shows one witness; what other witnesses to the same emergence would have seen is open.
- Whether gateway-marked mortals (those drained by proximity to imitation gateways) experience emergence differently. A productive open question for later fragments.
- Whether emergence is more or less depleted near imitation gateways. The brainstorm establishes this is the truth; the prologue holds it ambiguous in the rendering.

## Notes for the writer-agent

When future fragments render an emergence (or a god's recollection of their own emergence, or a mortal's mythologized account of one), use the canon above as a floor. The threshold / door / "color of closed eyelids" / animal stillness / first speech felt in the body should be motifs the reader recognizes across appearances. Different POVs may notice different elements, and the cultural mythologies the brainstorm describes will distort the account in different directions — that variance is part of the world.
