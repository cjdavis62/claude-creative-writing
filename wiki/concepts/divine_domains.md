# Divine Domains

A god's domain is the sphere of reality that its presence shapes and sustains. Not a power the god wields so much as a property of what the god *is* — the world around a god conforms to the nature of its domain the way water conforms to the shape of a vessel. Domains are not chosen by gods (as far as is understood); they are the gods' nature.

## Conscious vs. subconscious domain expression

The most important distinction in understanding domains, established in Fragment 5 (DECISIONS.md 2026-05-16):

**Conscious domain expression** is deliberate. The god knows what it is doing. It extends or applies its domain toward a specific end. The preservation god consciously extending its stasis outward at scale to freeze the volcanic eruption is conscious domain expression — a deliberate act under crisis, directed toward a specific outcome.

**Subconscious / ambient domain effects** operate without the god's active awareness. The world around a god simply conforms to its nature. The god may not know this is happening. The preservation god unknowingly holding back the volcanic forces beneath its city for generations — possibly centuries — is a subconscious domain effect. The god did not know there were forces to hold back. It was not doing this intentionally. Its presence was simply sufficient.

This distinction has enormous implications:

- A god's conscious domain capacity may be severely limited by decline while its ambient effects continue operating. The god may be unable to deliberately use its domain but still be shaping its territory subconsciously.
- A declining god may be unknowingly failing to maintain subconscious domain effects that mortals have taken for what they thought was the natural order. When those effects cease, the "natural" thing they were preventing or sustaining becomes visible for the first time.
- Gods themselves may not know what they are subconsciously maintaining. The preservation god did not know about the volcanic forces; there may have been no way for it to know. This is a different kind of tragedy than visible decline.

## What domains are not

**Not necessarily elemental.** Not all gods have elemental or nature-based domains (harvest, sea, war, fire). Some — particularly those in more intellectually complex cultures — have abstract domains: accord, witness, preservation, the continuation of existing states, the unfolding of natural processes. These are no less real than elemental domains; they are simply harder to name. Their ambient effects are correspondingly harder to attribute. A god whose domain is "the quality of resonance in a valley" produces an effect that visitors notice and cannot name; when it fails, the effect's absence is named only through its negative image (tinnitus).

**Not the same as power.** Domain is not simply what a god can do. It is what a god is. A god that has lost most of its conscious capacity through decline still has its domain — the ambient effects persist even past the god's waking. The domain is the last thing to go.

**Not fully understood by gods.** Gods know their domains in the way a person knows their own nature — incompletely, partially, through its effects rather than through direct introspection. A god may not know all of what its domain encompasses.

## Domain failure modes

Each domain fails in its own idiom. Some patterns:

- **Domains whose function is invisible succeed silently and fail loudly.** Accord and preservation are examples: when they work, nobody notices them; when they fail, the wrongness is unmistakable (social mis-rhythms; geological disaster). The tragedy is that nobody thought to be grateful.
- **Domains whose function is legible as exchange fail gradually and deniably.** Witness is an example: the ritual form persists; the failure is absorbed as participant error; the decline proceeds inside a structure of concealment.
- **Domains whose function is sensory fail as sensory loss or distortion.** Sound is an example: the resonance quality degrades; the tinnitus is the domain's broken signal persisting past the gift.

## The subconscious maintenance implication

The fragment dossier's most significant world-building development (Fragment 5, DECISIONS.md 2026-05-16): what mortals call natural facts — stable geology, reliable ecology, social coherence, the way sound moves in a place — may include subconscious divine maintenance so old and ambient that no one has thought to attribute it to a god. This reframes the entire crisis. The collapse of the divine plane is not only about losing what gods actively provided; it is about losing what gods passively maintained, without knowing they were doing so, and without mortals knowing anything was being maintained.

The question this raises — and that the main narrative will need to confront — is: how much of what the mortal world takes as the natural order is actually sustained by gods nobody remembers to name?
