# Wild Gods

Gods that exist outside of god–society relationships — neither patron to a community, nor member of a council, nor in a process of withdrawal toward some other arrangement. Wild gods have always been in the margins, or have been there long enough that the distinction between their original nature and their current state has become unclear even to them.

Wild gods are not failed gods. They are not gods in decline. They are gods that never entered (or have long since left) the reciprocal arrangement of patron-model or council-model life. Their relationship to mortals ranges from ambient indifference to active avoidance to simply not perceiving that mortals are present at all.

## The three types (readers of decline taxonomy)

This is the working taxonomy developed by readers of decline and those who study divine presence from the margins. The gods themselves do not use these categories.

### Almost-mythic

Gods old enough and long-absent enough from human interaction that they have become difficult to distinguish from the landscape. The almost-mythic wild god in the eastern margins is the canonical example. Effects:

- Sound, light, and other ambient phenomena go toward the god and do not return in the usual way. Not hostile absorption — simply the nature of something that old and that large.
- Navigation and spatial sense fail near the god's core territory. The body's locational knowledge stops working reliably.
- The god does not perceive mortals. Not deliberately — the category "mortal in my territory" does not register. The mortals are in the same ontological category as the wind.
- Proximity to an almost-mythic wild god suppresses proximity-marks from other gods (the tinnitus in Fragment 4) — but this is ambient effect, not reception. The god is not quieting anything; its presence simply overwhelms the finer signal.
- Cannot function as a patron. Not because they refuse, but because the relational structure does not exist in them. You cannot petition something that does not know you are there.

Almost-mythic wild gods are associated with stable wild margins communities: their territories have been shaped by the god's long presence, and communities that have lived in the margins long enough have developed practical knowledge of how to coexist with the effects without seeking exchange.

### Recently-gone-wild

A god that has left or been displaced from its territory within living memory — possibly one or two generations at most. Still coherent, still distinct from the landscape, still capable of something like a domain, but unmodulated. There is no community to be responsive to, no ritual to shape expression, no relationship that gives the domain a direction to move in. The domain expresses without constraint.

Recently-gone-wild gods are potentially more dangerous than almost-mythic ones — precisely because they are still present in the way a perceiving, expressing being is present. They may still respond to stimuli in ways that almost-mythic gods do not. Their responses are not calibrated to mortal needs or safety; they are not hostile, but the distinction may not matter practically.

The trajectory: a recently-gone-wild god that does not find a new territory or relationship will, over long periods, move toward simply-surviving and eventually toward almost-mythic. The timeline is not known.

### Simply-surviving

A god that has lost its domain — through decline, through the territory being fundamentally altered, through separation from its community — but has not yet been absorbed into the landscape. Still distinct, still a coherent presence, but without the domain expression that characterizes the other types. Waiting, perhaps, without knowing what for.

Simply-surviving gods are the rarest of the three types in the fragment dossier, and the least documented by readers of decline. They are harder to find (no domain effects to navigate by, no community to hear about them from) and harder to characterize when found. They are the future of a recently-gone-wild god that does not find new footing.

## What mortals want from wild gods

Desperate communities — particularly those whose patron gods have declined and who have heard rumors of wild gods that "don't decline" — occasionally attempt to find almost-mythic wild gods and petition them as replacement patrons. Fragment 4 documents this pattern. These attempts fail because:

1. The god does not perceive the petitioners.
2. Even if it did, it has no framework for the patron relationship — no structure through which to receive, respond, or give.
3. The ambient effects of the god's presence (sound absorption, navigation failure) are neither hostile nor welcoming; they simply are what they are.

The tragedy of Fragment 4 is not that the almost-mythic wild god refused. It is that the concept of refusal does not apply. The group experienced the god's ambient presence (tinnitus silenced, sound absorbed in one direction), interpreted it as reception and welcome, and were absorbed — not because the god acted on them, but because they moved toward where the sound was going and sound, once going in one direction, does not come back.

## Wild gods and the crisis

Wild gods are not affected by the imitation gateway problem in the same way as patron and council gods. Their lack of community relationship means they are not subject to the drain from gateway-building devotion in the direct way that embedded gods are. This may be why almost-mythic wild gods appear not to decline — they were never integrated into the mortal world's relationship to the divine in the first place.

This is a relevant data point that readers of decline have noted without fully understanding: communities near almost-mythic wild gods sometimes show unusual resilience. The explanation is not that the wild god is protecting them; it is that the wild god's ambient presence partially substitutes for the missing divine maintenance in ways that neither the community nor the god understands.

## Open questions

- Whether almost-mythic wild gods were always as they are, or were once patron or council gods that withdrew far enough to become landscape.
- Whether any wild god has ever been successfully brought back into a patron or council relationship, and what that would look like.
- Whether the simply-surviving type can be helped, and by whom.
