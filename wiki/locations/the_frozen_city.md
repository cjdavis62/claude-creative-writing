# The Frozen City

A mid-sized city — large enough to have a central quarter, multiple districts, and a functioning commercial life — that suffered a catastrophic divine event approximately two years before Fragment 5's present day. The event left the city physically altered in two distinct ways: a frozen district in which the preservation god's stasis domain is still operating, and a geologically changed landscape in the surrounding region where the incoming god redirected the main volcanic flow at the cost of its own life.

The city continues to function. Its response to the catastrophe has been practical: roped boundaries around the frozen district, administrative notices, property disputes over the inaccessible quarter. The grief has been organized.

## The Divine Event

The city had a long-resident **preservation god** whose domain was a gentle tendency toward the continuation of existing states. The god's domain operated ambient and below the level of its own awareness — it was not consciously holding back the dormant volcanic forces beneath the city, but its presence and nature meant those forces were contained. The god did not know it was doing this.

An **incoming god** (domain: natural processes unfolding — things becoming what they will become, forces moving toward their ends) arrived in the city for reasons not fully recorded. The incoming god did not know the city's domain was already held. Its presence — simply by being what it was — awakened the volcanic forces the preservation god had unconsciously suppressed.

Both gods responded to the same event from different positions:

- The **preservation god** extended its stasis outward at scale, freezing the eruption mid-flow. In doing so it depleted past waking and went into stasis itself. The domain continues to operate without the god's active consciousness.
- The **incoming god** redirected the main volcanic flow — the rock, heat, and gas that would have taken the city's central quarter — away from the population. The act required more than the god had. It died in the redirection.

The event was not a conflict between gods. They acted against the same force from different positions, with different tools, and the city survived because of both of them.

## The Frozen District

A portion of the city sealed in the preservation god's stasis at the moment of the eruption. Two years later, nothing inside the boundary has changed: wood has not rotted, plants are stopped mid-season with their color unfaded, objects sit exactly where they were. The district is bounded by rope and administrative notices. Entry is not formally permitted, and there is no record of anyone successfully crossing the boundary — though this has not been systematically tested.

The stasis is still running. This should not be possible if the god is in stasis; the domain is operating without its source. The implication — recognized by the historian-POV of Fragment 5, and not yet widely grasped — is that divine maintenance can persist beyond a god's active consciousness, and may have been doing so, invisibly, long before the event made it legible.

## Geological Alteration

The surrounding landscape — particularly to the south and west of the city — shows recent geological change: field settlement, altered grade, the edge of previously flat ground running at different angles. This is the trace of the incoming god's redirected flow. The volcanic forces went somewhere; they went somewhere not densely populated. The city was saved; the land around it was changed. Anyone who grew up here and has been away for even two years will notice it.

## The Preservation God — Current Status

In stasis. Depleted past waking. The domain runs without the god's direction. Whether the god can recover from this state is not established. The academic literature has begun to argue about what this means for the property law of the frozen district; the theological implications have not yet been formally addressed.
