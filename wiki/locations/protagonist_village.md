# The Protagonist's Village

The home village of the protagonist (the Mortal) and the site of the prologue. In the prologue, it is shown at evening on the day a god emerges on the slope above it — generations before the main story's present. By the main story's present day, the village is an absent-god society: the god of the prologue emergence is long gone, and the community has reorganized around that absence.

## Established texture (from `writing/fragments/prologue_emergence.md`)

- **Geography:** built into terraced slopes on a mountainside; a village proper below, terraces above for farming and grazing, "third wall" being the conventional upper limit children are warned not to cross. Above the third wall the slope changes — less grass, more stone, a few bent pines.
- **Above the village:** the slope continues up past collapsed old stone walls (older than living memory — "years before her mother was born") and a few wind-bent pines.
- **Below or behind:** a mountain blocks the view to the west; the village sits low enough that no horizon is visible from the slope above it.
- **Daily life:** agrarian and pastoral (goats are domestic livestock); has at least a smith (cookfire smoke is mentioned from the smith's house); evening cookfires; a packed-dirt lane; a culture in which children are sent to recover wandered goats and warned not to climb above the third wall.
- **Architecture:** packed-dirt lanes; houses with cookfires (chimneys or hearths visible by smoke); old stone walls — both the terrace walls and older collapsed ones suggesting the place is older than current habitation.

## Theological history

- A god emerged on the slope above the village in the prologue, generations before the main story's present.
- The village will eventually mythologize this emergence in many competing accounts ("they would all have different words"), with the actual first witness — the girl — displaced from the canonical version.
- Per the absent-god society model: by the present day, this god is presumably long-departed (declined, gone wild, dead, or otherwise absent), and the village operates around the legacy of their presence rather than the presence itself. This shapes the protagonist's relationship to divinity.

## Notes for the writer-agent

The protagonist was shaped by growing up here. Her gritty, unillusioned narration is partly the residue of growing up in a community whose patron god has been gone long enough that the rituals are habit and the theology is contested. When the village appears in present-day fragments, render it as a place with stratigraphic memory — older walls beneath newer ones, older names beneath newer ones, an emergence story everyone knows differently.
