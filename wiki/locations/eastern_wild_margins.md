# The Eastern Wild Margins

The territory east of the settled regions, beyond the reach of maintained roads and organised human use. The eastern wild margins are not a single defined place — they are everything past where the roads end, where the land has not been organized long enough for humans to name its parts. They are reached from the sound valley by approximately seventeen days' travel east, with the road ending around day nine.

The almost-mythic wild god lives here — has lived here long enough that the margins are shaped around its presence. The territory is not the god's domain in the patron-model sense; the god has no domain in that sense. The territory is simply where the god is, and where the god is, the world has adjusted.

## Approach and Transition

The approach to the wild margins is gradual. There is no marked border — the land changes, but not in ways that are easy to name. The transition the group in Fragment 4 experienced:

- **Day 9 or so:** The road ends. Not through damage or neglect — it simply stops, the way roads do in places where human use has not persisted long enough to maintain one. After this point, direction is held by feel and approximation.
- **Two days before the god's territory:** Birds becoming quieter, then absent. The ordinary busy noise of undergrowth — the small sounds of a living forest — ceasing. Wind still present, leaves still moving, but something missing from the texture of how sound moves through trees. Sound going somewhere and not returning.
- **Day 16 onward:** Body's knowledge of location begins to fail. Navigation is possible by visible landmarks (the sun, the direction), but arrival keeps coming as a mild surprise. Where you reach is not quite where you aimed for. This is not disorientation — it is something more precise: the dissolution of the animal sureness that says *this way, not there, you are here.*

## The God's Territory

The almost-mythic wild god's territory is phenomenologically distinct from ordinary wilderness. The effects:

**Sound:** Everything goes toward the god and does not return. Wind still moves, footsteps still land, voices still leave — but nothing echoes. The world accepts what is offered and gives nothing back. This is not silence; it is sound with one direction removed.

**Navigation:** The body's locational sense — the deep spatial knowledge that does not require reasoning — stops working reliably. You can see where you are; you cannot feel where you are. These are different things, and their separation is disorienting in a way that precedes any conscious response to it.

**The tinnitus mark:** For bearers of the sound god's proximity-mark, the tinnitus ceases in the god's territory. The almost-mythic wild god's ambient presence is sufficient to suppress the broken signal. This suppression was experienced by the Fragment 4 group as confirmation — the rumor was right, a god is near, the ringing has stopped. It was also misleading: the suppression is proximity-effect, not reception. The god does not know they are there.

## The Almost-Mythic Wild God

See also `wiki/concepts/wild_gods.md` (to be created).

The god in the eastern margins is classified as *almost-mythic* — it has been in the wild long enough that it has become difficult to distinguish from the landscape itself. It is not hostile. It does not perceive the group. It is simply old enough and large enough that sound goes toward it and does not return, that the land adjusts around its presence, that proximity to it suppresses proximity-marks from other gods.

**What it is not:** It is not a patron. It does not receive, does not give, does not attend. The displaced communities who attempt to find wild gods as replacement patrons fail because ancient wild gods do not function as patrons — not because they refuse, but because the category does not apply to them. They cannot be asked anything. There is nothing to ask.

**What happened to the group:** Three of the six who reached the god's territory were absorbed — sound went toward them as it went toward everything else, and did not return. They walked toward where the sound was going. They became suggestions, and then simply the trees. The god did not act on them; it did not notice them; their absorption was the same process as the absorption of birdsong and wind. Only the one who read the directionality of the sound correctly — who understood that the silence was not welcome, not reception — turned back.

## Relationship to Other Wild God Types

The eastern wild god is one of at least three types of wild god the world contains:

- **Almost-mythic:** Landscape-absorbed, old enough that the distinction between the god and its territory has become unclear. The eastern margins god. Does not perceive mortals; cannot function as a patron.
- **Recently-gone-wild:** A god that has left or been displaced from its territory within living memory. Still coherent, still capable of something like a domain, but unmodulated — no community to be responsive to, no ritual to shape expression. More dangerous than almost-mythic because more present.
- **Simply-surviving:** A god that has lost its domain but is still distinct from the landscape. Waiting, not absorbed. The future of a recently-gone-wild god that does not find a new territory.

*These distinctions are the working taxonomy of readers of decline; they are not formal categories the gods themselves recognize.*
