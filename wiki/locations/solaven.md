# Solaven

A patron-model settlement. Name derives from the patron-culture compound form: *sol-* (kept, held) + *-aven* (place of) — "the kept place." The name encodes the community's relationship to its god: a place where things are held, where the chain of continuity is maintained.

## Physical character

Solaven is organised around a single patron god whose domain is witness and continuity. The god's ritual space — the **Tavorael** ("where the god receives," from *tavor-* reception/arrival + *-ael* divine suffix) — is the community's centre of gravity. Most civic life is calibrated around the witness-season and its associated rituals.

## The witness ritual

A seasonal community ritual in which members come forward to the speaking-stone to present something to the god's presence: a name, a loss, a year's work, a child newly born. The god receives it — or did. The god's domain is witness and permanence: when it receives what is brought, the name or loss or event is held beyond the reach of forgetting, kept by something that does not die. The exchange is: *I give you this, and you keep it where things don't get lost.* When functioning, the exchange produces a perceptible shift in the room that participants feel as confirmation — the name has left them, received. When it fails, the name returns to the speaker: still theirs to carry, ungiven. The failure is felt as the participant's own inadequacy rather than the god's absence; they assume they spoke wrong, that they need to return.

## Current status

The witness-ritual's transaction is failing. A reader of decline (Tavoen) has tracked the count across three years, recording failed exchanges: 4 of 67 failed (year one); 13 of 53 failed (year two); 35 of 41 failed (year three). The pattern is a clear escalation — fewer people coming, and a dramatically rising proportion receiving nothing. However: in the third year, one participant (a girl of approximately twelve) received a full response from the god when nearly forty others did not. This suggests the decline is not yet uniform and that residual divine presence responds differently to those at the thresholds of life. See `wiki/concepts/emergence.md` for the threshold-of-life principle.

## Road connections

Solaven is connected to the western settlements — including Vanaek — via the eastern road. Commercial couriers (including Beket, a long-running courier on this route) pass through the region. The same road network that carries commercial traffic also carries the informal reports that orient readers of decline: a story about a ritual not landing right, a witness who came back twice, the feel of a room that wasn't quite working. Tavoen's presence in Solaven is consistent with how readers of decline typically identify sites — word of mouth, traveler reports, the texture of what arrives along these roads.

## Notes

Solaven is a patron-model society and represents the most common god–society configuration. Its trajectory under decline — the ritual persisting in form while losing its substance, participants internalising the failure as their own — is one of the canonical shapes of how patron societies unravel.
