# The Sound Valley

The home settlement of the displaced group in Fragment 4. A valley community that was formerly a patron-model society organized around a **sound god** — a god whose domain gave the valley a distinctive quality of resonance. The god has declined past the point of viability. The community, some of its members bearing permanent proximity-marks from the decline, attempted to reach a wild god in the eastern margins as a replacement patron. That attempt failed. Whether the settlement itself continues to function is not established.

## The Sound God's Domain

The sound god's domain produced a quality of resonance in the valley that visitors noted and could not name: a particular way voices fell in the square, a depth to ordinary sound that made the valley feel more present than other places. This was not a performance or a ritual effect — it was the ambient expression of the god's nature in its territory. It was simply how sound worked here.

As the god declined, this quality degraded and eventually failed. What remained was its negative image: **tinnitus**. The god's domain, unable to sustain its gift, instead marked its people with the broken ghost of what it had been. The ringing is permanent. It is the sound god's domain written into the bodies of its bearers, transmitting the broken intervals of what the domain had been before it turned on itself.

## Proximity-marks and Their Distribution

The tinnitus is not uniform. The marks are shaped by proximity and by origin:

- Those born nearest the domain-hall carry the worst of the ringing. In at least one case, a woman born within sight of the hall had begun to hear patterns in the noise — rhythms, the ghost of intervals, the broken music of what the god's domain had been.
- Those born farther away or who came to the valley later carry a lighter version.
- Proximity to a functioning god suppresses the tinnitus temporarily. The group in Fragment 4 experienced the silencing of the ringing upon approaching the almost-mythic wild god — and its return as they withdrew.
- The suppression and return confirmed the permanence of the mark: proximity to any sufficiently present divine being can quiet it, but only for as long as proximity is maintained. It comes back.

## The Displacement Attempt

Nine community members set out east, following a rumor that a god in the eastern margins did not decline. The rumor had the quality of desperation: vague enough to be plausible, specific enough to travel — a cousin's husband's report of a settlement that stood through three bad winters, that did not lose anyone to a fever that took thirty from the valley. The rumored god was described as old enough to have become the landscape.

The journey east took seventeen days, with significant attrition:

- One older man sat down at the side of the road on day four and did not get up. They left him food and water. They did not discuss it.
- One member fell into a ravine on day fourteen. The sound of it was wrong.
- Three others were lost to the god's absorption — sound went toward them and did not return.

Six reached the god's territory. The POV character was the only one to turn back.

## Current Status

Unknown. The settlement presumably continues in some form, but it is no longer a functioning patron-model society — the god is gone, the mark is permanent, and the displacement attempt failed. Whether the community has reorganized around absence (as absent-god-model societies do) or dispersed is not established.
