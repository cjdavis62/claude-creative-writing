# The Wild Margins Model

Communities that exist at or near the edges of settled territory — in proximity to wild gods — and have developed modes of life shaped by that proximity rather than by a traditional patron or council relationship. Not a society built *around* the god (as patron or council models are) but one that has found an accommodation with something that does not attend to them.

## What distinguishes wild margins communities

Wild margins communities are not organized around their god in the way patron or council societies are. The almost-mythic wild gods in their vicinity do not patronize, do not receive offerings, do not participate in ritual exchange, and often do not perceive the community at all. The community's adaptation is therefore not one of devotion or dependency but of **coexistence with an ambient presence** — learning to live near something vast without expecting anything from it.

This produces a distinctive social character:

**Pragmatic fatalism.** Wild margins communities tend toward a less transactional relationship with the divine than patron societies. The god does not answer. This is simply true. Communities that remain near wild gods over generations develop a cultural posture that accepts divine indifference as a baseline rather than a failure state.

**Environmental knowledge.** Living near an almost-mythic wild god produces specific, hard-won knowledge of the god's ambient effects — sound behavior, animal movement, the navigation failures near the god's core territory, which plants thrive and which don't, which paths are safe at different seasons. This knowledge is practical, granular, and untranslatable to outsiders who lack the baseline experience. Wild margins communities often seem cryptic to visitors asking about "the god"; what they know is not theology but geography.

**Complex relationship to the idea of patronage.** Communities that hear about patron societies and their gods — the intimacy, the exchange, the warmth — may feel something between envy and pity. They have something vast. It does not notice them. Whether this is better or worse than the patron model's devotion and dependence is a live question within wild margins culture.

## The desperate pilgrimage problem

Wild margins communities and the routes through their territory sometimes attract desperate groups — communities whose patron gods have declined, who have heard rumors that a wild god in the margins doesn't decline, who attempt to find and petition the god for patronage.

These attempts fail. Almost-mythic wild gods do not function as patrons — not because they refuse, but because the category does not apply to them. They cannot be asked anything. They do not perceive the petitioners.

Wild margins communities that have watched this pattern repeat develop a tragic knowledge: the pilgrims come believing the rumors (the god is old, the god doesn't decline, the settlement nearby has endured three bad winters), travel through the margins toward the core territory, and do not return, or return changed, or — as in Fragment 4 — some turn back and some don't. The wild margins communities are neither guides nor rescuers; they are witnesses to a recurring tragedy.

## Proximity effects and wild margins life

Living near a wild god produces its own proximity effects. These are not the domain-specific marks that patron gods leave on their communities; they are the ambient effects of something very old and very large being nearby:

- The navigation instability of the god's core territory is known and mapped informally; experienced residents learn to allow for it.
- Sound behavior may vary near the margins in ways that are simply accepted as local weather — the way sound falls here, the way echoes do or don't work.
- Animals in wild margins communities may be permanently altered in behavior — not distressed, but recalibrated to the god's ambient presence in ways that make them behave strangely when taken to settled territory.

## The Eastern Wild Margins

The region featured in Fragment 4. See `wiki/locations/eastern_wild_margins.md`. The settlement the Fragment 4 group was rumored to be near — "a settlement that stood through three bad winters" — is a wild margins community that has achieved a stable coexistence with the almost-mythic god's ambient maintenance of its territory.

## Notes for future scenes

The wild margins register is rarely directly visited in the main narrative — the protagonist's journey is through the settled world and its decline. But wild margins communities and their long coexistence with divine indifference are a useful counterpoint to the patron model's expectation of exchange. A scene in a wild margins community would show what divine presence without divine relationship looks like, and how communities build meaning in the absence of the intimacy the rest of the world has organized around.
