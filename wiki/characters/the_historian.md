# The Historian

The POV character of Fragment 5 (*The City After*). A researcher who studies divine power and decline, returning to the city where they grew up after two years away, specifically to examine the frozen district left by a catastrophic divine event. Studies the district from the boundary rope — does not enter.

## What is established

**Background:** Grew up in the frozen city. Left approximately two years before Fragment 5's present day — the departure preceded the divine event, or coincided with its immediate aftermath, and was committed to enough that they kept going when they heard news of the event (the archive was expecting them, and the reports reached them by road). The choice to keep going rather than return is not directly rendered in the fragment but is legible in the opening: *she had been three weeks east when she heard. She had kept going.*

**Profession:** A historian or researcher focused on divine power and decline. The work involves archives, first-hand accounts, and systematic study of how gods' domains operated and failed. Has access to primary sources, including private accounts like the factor's clerk's letter and the god-attendant's testimony — suggesting either institutional access or a well-developed personal network.

**Emotional register:** Scholarly distance over personal grief — the academic frame as a coping mechanism. The fragment maintains this discipline throughout, with a single crack at the end: she does not write anything down. A historian who has been processing everything through the scholarly frame for two years, standing at the boundary of her childhood city's frozen district, finally fails at the one thing historians do.

**Relationship to the city:** Personal. The field south of the road — its altered grade after the divine event — is something she would only have noticed if she grew up looking at it from cart windows, year by year. The frozen district's streets, the building with the window box, the corner with the short-legged table — these are places she knew. The scholarly frame is not detachment from the city; it is what makes return survivable.

**What she discovers:** That the preservation god's stasis domain is still running two years after the god entered stasis. And the larger implication that follows: divine maintenance may be everywhere and invisible, and the god's ambient effects may have been holding back forces nobody knew were being held. See [[divine_domains]] and [[the_frozen_city]].

## What is not established

- Name, pronouns (withheld in fragment; she/her used provisionally here)
- Whether she was in the city when the event occurred, or had already left
- What she does with her discovery — whether she publishes, shares with readers of decline, or keeps it
- Whether she recurs in the main narrative
- Her relationship to the readers of decline network (she has access to primary accounts that suggest connections, but whether she is formally affiliated is unknown)
- What the two years of research before her return produced, and whether it informs how she reads the frozen district

## Potential narrative function

The historian's position — someone who grew up in this world, has developed a scholarly framework for understanding divine decline, and has just made the most significant discovery the fragment dossier contains — makes her a natural candidate for a recurring figure in the main narrative. She is not the protagonist, but she may be someone the protagonist encounters: a person who has figured out a piece of the problem that the protagonist needs, embedded in a city whose disaster the protagonist passes through.

Whether she recurs is an author decision, deferred.
