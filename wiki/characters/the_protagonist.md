# The Protagonist

The main POV of the novel. A woman from an absent-god-model village, gateway-marked from early in her life, whose narrowed and unillusioned perception is the lens through which the story's ugliness is most visible — and is also, unknown to her, evidence of the mechanism destroying the divine plane.

## What she wants

**Surface want:** To kill the god she encounters at the tree. A specific, charged desire rooted in personal history. Not generic god-hatred — something pointed.

**Middle want:** To fulfill the quest despite herself. She does not want to care about it. She presses forward anyway, and cannot name why.

**Deep need:** To understand that her perception of the world — which she experiences as clarity — is itself a wound. And that the wound is also what makes her uniquely capable of seeing the truth at the end. These two things are the same thing; the story is about her coming to hold them as the same thing.

## The gateway-mark

She is gateway-marked. Born near or significantly exposed to an imitation gateway, she has been slowly drained her whole life. The drain has produced a narrowing of perception — a loss of the capacity for idealism, for beauty in the register the dying deity moves in, for the kind of trust that doesn't have a practical argument behind it.

She does not know this. Her inability to see ideals or beauty in the world is not, to her, a symptom — it is simply her. It has been confirmed over and over by the world she has actually lived in, which makes it nearly impossible for her to distinguish between *what the drain has done* and *what the world has shown her*.

The dying deity sees the mark on her and partially misreads it — interpreting her stripped-down perception as useful clarity rather than recognizing it as evidence of the drain's effect. The choice to entrust her with the quest is the most correct thing the dying deity does. The reasoning behind it is incomplete.

## Voice and register

Gritty. Unillusioned. Occasionally bitter. Her narration is body-first and specific — she notices what is actually in front of her rather than what things mean. She is not cynical in a performed way; she has simply stopped expecting things to be other than they are.

What she cannot easily perceive: beauty that requires a certain openness to receive (the dying deity's register), idealism that hasn't been earned through loss, the appeal of structures of meaning she has already watched fail. What she perceives unusually clearly: the texture of how things actually work, the gap between what people say and what they do, the specific physical facts of a situation.

This perceptual profile is both narrower and sharper than an unmarked person's. The dying deity reads only the sharper half.

## Arc

She begins the novel not believing in the quest — not in the dying deity's ability to entrust it correctly, not in her own capacity to carry it, not in whatever the quest is supposed to accomplish. She completes it out of a compulsion she cannot name: something in her presses forward even when her reasoning argues against it. At the moment of the last god's emergence, she feels the drain consciously for the first time — recognizes in her body what the dying deity's investigation approached from the outside and never quite reached. The recognition is not triumph. It is the specific terrible clarity of understanding what you are at the moment when it no longer matters to deny it.

## Backstory wound

The specific wound that produced her worldview is not yet settled in canon. Per PROJECT_PLAN.md §4.2, it should not be sexual violence — both because that is the over-relied-on backstory for women in fantasy and because it would shift the story's thematic weight in ways that work against its actual concerns. The wound should be something that specifically confirms an unillusioned view of the world — a loss or betrayal that makes idealism feel like a lie rather than a luxury. This is an author decision, deferred.

## Relationship to the dying deity

The relationship is the novel's central asymmetry. He sees something in her that he cannot fully name. She is resistant, skeptical, not interested in being chosen for anything. What eventually forms between them is not faith on her part but something more grudging: a provisional, practical trust that he is at least in earnest, which is more than she extends to most. His death at the tree is the event that puts the quest in motion; his flashback chapters are his attempt to explain himself to her across the temporal gap.

## Home and origin

The protagonist's home village: see `wiki/locations/protagonist_village.md`. An absent-god society — the god of the prologue was gone long before her time. The stratigraphic memory of the village (older walls, contested emergence stories, rituals that have become habit rather than transaction) is part of what shaped her relationship to the divine. For her, the gods' absence is simply how things are.
