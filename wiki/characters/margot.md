# Margot

A goat. Old by the time of the prologue. Belongs to the witness girl's family in the protagonist's village. Has been with the family longer than the witness can remember — *grazed the lower terraces every day I had been alive.*

## Established texture (from `prologue_emergence.md` v4)

- **Old.** "Old Margot." Old enough that her age is part of her name.
- **Scarred.** Her right horn was broken before the witness was born; she scratches her left horn against a wall when she has an itch.
- **Has habits.** A specific place she lies down at midday with her old eyes half-closed. A specific spot by the fallen-block where she grazes. A place at the second terrace's wall where the soil goes soft.
- **Stubborn.** Eats first. Eats even when there are wolves. Will not lift her head for anything in normal circumstance — pa's saying about her, retold by the girl: *if she was going to be a wolf's meal, she'd be damned if she were to miss her own.*
- **Argues with directions.** Goats do not hold a direction. Margot is a goat among goats — except on the night of the emergence.

## Margot on the night of the emergence

- Walks straight off the worn track and up the slope, holding a single direction with uncharacteristic intensity. Does not pause at her usual places. Does not stop to graze.
- The first witness of the emergence — arrives at the site before the god, and stands head-up, perfectly still, looking at empty air.
- Bleats at the sky as the membrane begins to break.
- Returns to grazing only after the god is fully present and has acknowledged the girl — the moment when (in animal-perception) the threat or attention has resolved.

## What happens to Margot after

She is left on the slope by the witness girl, who runs home alone. Four days later, Margot comes down from the high pasture in pa's company, alongside the man who emerged. She is alive. She is changed. She never grazes the lower terraces again.

Margot is one of the *unmythologized changed beings* of the prologue's closing. The village will have words for the man on the slope; the village will have no words for what changed in Margot.

## Held open

- What Margot becomes — quieter? attentive in a new way? does she take to following the god? does she avoid the girl?
- What killed her right horn before the girl was born — pa's story, presumably; not yet established.
- Whether Margot lives long enough to appear in the main story (she is already old at the time of the prologue and there is a significant temporal gap to the present day; probably no, but not impossible).

## Notes for the writer-agent

Margot is a character. She has a body. She has habits. She has a name. The prose treats her with the same standards as the human characters per `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §4.4 — she does not exist to serve the narrative, she has her own life inside it. When future fragments render the witness girl as an old woman, Margot may be a remembered presence in her — old goats remembered, not symbolized.
