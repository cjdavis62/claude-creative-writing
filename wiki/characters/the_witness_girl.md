# The Witness Girl

A girl, approximately seven to nine years old at the time of the prologue, from the protagonist's home village. She crossed the third wall above the village's terraced slopes one evening looking for a wandered goat and witnessed a god's emergence — door, glimpse of an impossible horizon, the god himself arriving disoriented and not yet seeing her.

She is *not* the protagonist as a child. She is a separate person, who will later appear in the story — likely as an older woman, possibly a meaningful character, possibly a passing reference. The temporal gap between the prologue and the main story's present is significant.

## What canon says about her so far

- Lives in the protagonist's home village (an absent-god society in the present day, per the brainstorm)
- Old enough to have been told twice already to come home before dark; old enough to defy that
- Has at least one parent: a mother
- Was not noticed by the god during the emergence (he saw her only after his arrival; she was already deciding to call him "a man")
- After the emergence, found unable to speak about what she saw
- The village's eventual mythology of the emergence is built without her account — "they would all have different words. None of them would be hers"

## Held open

- Name (the brainstorm holds names until the linguistic logic clarifies)
- What she becomes by the time the protagonist encounters her
- Whether she ever found language for what she saw
- Whether the god's arrival left her gateway-marked (the prologue is silent on this; she may simply have been too far away, or not)
- Whether she is alive in the present-day of the main story

## Notes for the writer-agent

She is loadbearing for the project's commitment to character agency: she existed before the emergence, she exists after, and her own life continues whether the gods are watching or not. When she returns to the page later, she should arrive as a person with her own years between, not as a footnote to the prologue. See `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §4.4.
