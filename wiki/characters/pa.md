# Pa

The witness girl's father. Offstage during the prologue itself — at the high pasture working the herd, two days from home. Comes down four days after the night of the emergence, in the company of the man who emerged on the slope, and with Old Margot.

## Established texture (from `prologue_emergence.md` v4)

- **Herder.** Manages the family herd. Was at the high pasture during the prologue. Has been close enough to wolves to "rush the herd away from a wolf pack last spring."
- **Wry. Gallows humor.** Has sayings, retold by the girl. About Margot: *if she was going to be a wolf's meal, she'd be damned if she were to miss her own.* He says things like this in front of his daughter, who is not supposed to know the words but does.
- **A teacher.** When pa needed his daughter to understand not to cross the third wall, he put his hand on the back of her neck, gently, and turned her face toward the wall and said *you understand?* until she said *I understand* back to him. Firm parenting, no violence.
- **Steady stillness.** The girl knows the way pa goes still when he hears a sound he does not like. She uses this as a reference for how Margot stands at the moment of the emergence: *Margot was still. Still the way pa was still when he heard a sound he did not like.*

## Pa and the god

Four days after the night of the emergence, pa comes home from the high pasture. He is not alone. The man who emerged on the slope walks beside him, and Old Margot is with them. Pa will not say where he picked up the company.

This becomes the village's *canonical* moment of the god's arrival — the moment the new presence comes down into the village in the herder's company. The actual moment on the slope, witnessed by the girl, is never given canonical place in the mythology. (See `prologue_emergence.md` closing.)

## Notes for the writer-agent

Pa is offstage in the prologue but his voice is in the prose — through the girl's remembered sayings and reference points. The technique (per `STYLE_AND_VOICE.md`'s voice exemplar) is to bring family onstage through the child's *memory and reference*, never through narrator-summary.

When pa appears directly in future fragments, he should be a wry, watchful man whose sense of humor about death is real, not stylized — the gallows humor of someone who has worked with animals and knows the shape of loss. The herder culture should sit on him without being ornamental.
