# Tavoen

Working name used in the field. Real name unknown. A reader of decline operating in the patron-model society of Solaven.

## What is established

- Has visited Solaven three times over three years, tracking the witness-ritual. Each visit: different garment, different inn, different entry point into the Tavorael. Professional precautions that have become habit.
- Their methodology is numerical and qualitative simultaneously: they count participants, track whether the god's reception is felt, note the duration and quality of each speaker's wait. The record is held in memory during the ritual, written afterward in a merchant's ledger in the network's shorthand, in ink that fades after a month.
- Fanatical: the work runs as a second track underneath everything, including near-exposure. They stayed for the last speaker under active risk because thirty-four of forty-one was incomplete. Thirty-five of forty-one was the full year.
- During the Fragment 3 visit they documented a significant anomaly: the god responded to a twelve-year-old girl — shifted the room, briefly — after failing to respond to forty adults. The distinction between who receives and who doesn't may be important canon (connected to the threshold-of-life sensitivity established in the prologue).
- Nearly discovered by a young priest who recognised their angle of attention as a watcher's rather than a participant's. Extracted themselves at ordinary pace.

## What is withheld

- Real name
- How they entered the network
- What the network does with the information they gather
- Whether they were ultimately identified after Fragment 3

## Notes

The obsessive quality of Tavoen's work makes them a potential foil for the protagonist — who also can't stop seeing what others don't, but experiences that as wound rather than vocation. Tavoen has made the compulsion into a life's work. The protagonist hasn't yet made anything of it at all.
