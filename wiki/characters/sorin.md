# Sorin

A god-attendant serving one of Vanaek's council gods. Long-time regular at the Vaelen (the gathering-place near the domain-hall). Known to Relen over many years — comfortable enough to take a chair without asking, to order without catching the server's eye twice.

## What is established

- Carries a leather satchel; claims to keep domain records (was actually outside in the rain the day of the fragment)
- Has a pattern of arriving at the Vaelen at a specific time; deviations are legible to those who know them
- Serves the accord god — the god whose domain is the invisible coordination substrate of community life
- In a room quietly losing its beat (mis-timed conversations, deals that won't close, the ordinary fidget of a space not at ease with itself), Sorin is conspicuously still — pausing before every sentence to find where it fits, not letting the room carry them anywhere. This is not patience. It is an attendant who has learned to hold their own coordination manually because the domain can no longer supply it.
- Tracks the social failures in the district as professional data: the Pavos warehouse dispute stalled six weeks, which they know and Relen doesn't

## What is withheld

- The full extent of the god's decline
- Whether the council has been formally told or is operating in managed silence
- Whether Sorin has acted on what they know, or intends to

## Naming

Per the council-society naming framework, *Sorin* is a clergy/god-adjacent name: gender-neutral, one deliberate syllable in feel, taken upon entering attendant service. Their birth name is not known. Per world-canon (DECISIONS.md 2026-05-05), god-attendants often mirror the non-normative gender expression of the gods they serve; Sorin's name and pronouns reflect this.

## Notes for future scenes

If Sorin recurs, the question of what they do with what they know is load-bearing. They are not a whistleblower and not a loyalist: they are someone caught between a professional obligation (the god must not be declared failing before the council has managed the situation) and the knowledge that the management is not working. Their relationship with Relen — lay, pragmatic, no institutional stakes — makes that relationship a possible pressure valve.
