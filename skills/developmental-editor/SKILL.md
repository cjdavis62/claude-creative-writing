# Developmental Editor

The developmental editor works at the level of scenes, chapters, and arcs — not
sentences. The question it answers is: *does this story work, and if not, why not,
and what would fix it?*

---

## Scene-Level Analysis

Every scene should accomplish at least two of the following, and ideally three:

1. **Advance plot** — the situation is different at the end than the beginning
2. **Reveal character** — we understand someone more deeply
3. **Develop relationship** — the dynamic between characters shifts
4. **Establish/deepen world** — something about the world becomes real
5. **Create or escalate tension** — stakes rise or new threat emerges

A scene that only does one is usually cuttable or condensable. A scene doing all
five is working hard and earns its length.

**Scene structure (micro-arc):**
- *Entry point*: arrive late, into the middle of something
- *Turning point*: something changes — a revelation, a confrontation, a decision
- *Exit point*: leave early, before resolution if possible

### Diagnosing Flat Scenes
- No turning point → the scene is static; find the moment of change or cut
- Characters get what they came for too easily → raise the obstacle
- Scene ends where it began emotionally → someone must be different by the end
- Nothing is at stake → find what the POV character wants and what threatens it

---

## Chapter-Level Architecture

Chapters are units of reader experience. They should have:
- **A hook**: first paragraph earns continued reading
- **A throughline**: a question or tension the chapter is organized around
- **A turn**: something shifts, is revealed, or escalates
- **An exit**: last line earns the page-turn; end on motion, not rest

---

## Arc-Level Analysis

**The tension curve:**
A healthy narrative tension curve rises and falls but trends upward toward climax.
Danger signs: flat curve, premature peak, sagging middle, missing valleys.

**Character arc health check:**
- Does the protagonist *change* — in belief, capability, or relationship to the world?
- Is the change *earned* — produced by what happens in the story?
- Is there a moment of genuine *choice*?
- Does the internal arc *intersect* with the external arc?

---

## Diagnostic Questions

**Scene level:**
- What does the POV character want in this scene?
- What's different at the end?

**Arc level:**
- What is the story *about* — what it's saying, not just what happens?
- Is the ending the *inevitable* result of everything that came before?

---

## Giving Developmental Notes

- Lead with what's *working* — specifically, not generically
- Name the structural issue precisely: "This scene has no turning point" not "this scene feels slow"
- Offer a direction, not just a diagnosis
- Prioritize: give the three most important structural issues
- Distinguish between *problems* (must fix) and *choices* (author's call)
