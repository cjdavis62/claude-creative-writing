# Concept & Thematic Art Prompts

Prompts for DALL-E / ChatGPT. Style target: **ink and watercolor illustration** — loose, evocative, atmospheric. These prompts lean more abstract and symbolic than the character studies, suited to establishing mood and cosmological texture.

---

## Emergence

### The Threshold

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of a field at dusk. In the center of the image, a threshold stands in the open air — upright, door-shaped, roughly as wide as two outstretched arms and taller than a person. It is not made of anything. It is simply a thinning of the air in the shape of a door. Through it, a glimpse of somewhere else: a horizon that does not match the surrounding landscape, light along it that moves differently, a sky of an uncertain color. At the base of the threshold, several goats stand absolutely still, facing it — not afraid, not grazing. Waiting. The surrounding field is rendered in muted watercolor, the threshold itself in fine ink linework against pale, luminous wash. The palette is blue-grey dusk, pale gold at the threshold, the dark forms of the goats.

**What this captures:** The emergence event — the membrane giving way, the animal witnesses arriving first. The door as something that is not made but simply is.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (3:2)

### First Witness

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of a child standing in a field, looking up at something. She is young — seven or eight — and her body language is very still, the way children go still when something is outside their categories. Around her feet: goat hoofprints in soft earth. Just above the horizon line before her, the suggestion of a threshold that is already closing — a shimmer, a fold in the air, more felt than seen. At the edge of the frame, very faint, the suggestion of a figure who has just arrived: immaculate clothing, hands held slightly away from the body, eyes that are just beginning to focus. The child is the center of the image. The threshold and the arriving figure are peripheral. Loose watercolor in pale morning light; fine ink detail on the child's expression — not afraid. Present. The palette is cool grey-blue with pale gold where the threshold was.

**What this captures:** The prologue scene — the child as first human witness, the god arriving disoriented, the world just after the membrane closes.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (3:2)

## Decline

### The Accord Domain Failing

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of a crowded marketplace or gathering hall, rendered in a slightly dissonant way: people are present, conversations are happening, commerce is occurring — but none of it is landing. Two figures reach to shake hands at a slightly wrong moment. A third person speaks to a fourth who responds a beat too late. A deal that should have closed three days ago still has not closed. The scene looks busy from a distance; up close, it is quietly, unnamably off. The palette is warm amber and ochre — a living, busy place — but the ink linework draws out the gaps: the pauses, the missed beats, the conversations that keep not finding their rhythm. No supernatural effect is visible. The decline is entirely social, entirely texture.

**What this captures:** Middle decline of the accord god — domain-specific failure as environmental texture before anyone has language for it.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (3:2)

### The Ritual That Does Not Land

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of a religious ceremony in a patron-model hall. Figures stand in a loose circle; candles burn; a priest-figure leads something. The scene is dignified, practiced. But at its center, something is missing: the quality of exchange that should be present — a sense of reception, of response, of the ceremony completing — is simply absent. The participants hold their posture. The ritual proceeds. No one names what is not there. The image should convey ceremony that has become hollow without any single element announcing it. Loose, muted watercolor; ink detail on the priest's hands, the candle flames, the postures of those present. The palette is deep blue and gold, with the gold slightly drained, slightly less than it should be.

**What this captures:** The witness god's decline in Solaven — the ritual completing without its characteristic exchange.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (3:2)

## The Gateway Mechanic

### The Imitation Gateway

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of an ancient sacred site — a stone threshold set into a hillside, carved with devotional marks, worn smooth by generations of hands. Offerings are arranged at its base. Someone kneels before it in prayer. The threshold is real, well-made, clearly the result of love and craft and faith. But from above — a slightly elevated perspective — the scene shows something the worshipper cannot see: the air above the threshold trembles very faintly, moving not toward the divine but away from it, a slow and invisible bleed. The worshipper does not notice. Nothing looks wrong from the ground. The painting holds both truths at once: an act of sincere devotion, and the mechanism of catastrophe. Watercolor in warm amber for the devotion; fine ink for the threshold's carved detail; a faint, almost invisible distortion at the air above it.

**What this captures:** The central tragedy of the world — the imitation gateways built out of love, which are the mechanism of destruction. The irony that the worshipper cannot see from the ground.

**Aspect ratio:** Portrait (2:3)

### The Drain — Portrait of a Gateway-Marked Person

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor portrait of a figure standing near an ancient stone threshold — close, close enough for it to matter. The figure looks ordinary: plainly dressed, tired from travel, not performing anything. But the palette of the image is doing something: the colors around the threshold are slightly richer than they should be, and the colors around the figure are slightly less. Not dramatically — not visibly, from a distance — but the figure's world is fractionally more stripped, more grey, more drained than the world a step away from the gateway would be. The figure looks at the threshold with an expression that is not sacred or reverent — simply factual. They are reporting what they see, and what they see is stone. Fine ink detail; loose watercolor with a careful palette gradient.

**What this captures:** The gateway-mark as something that happens over time, invisibly, producing a narrowing of perception that the marked person experiences not as damage but as simply seeing clearly.

**Aspect ratio:** Portrait (2:3)

## Wild Gods

### The Almost-Mythic — Landscape as God

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor landscape illustration of a vast eastern wilderness — deep forest, high ground, a valley where sound is wrong. In the far distance, something large is suggested: not a figure exactly, not a shape exactly, more a quality of the terrain — a part of the landscape that is denser, older, more present than the rest, and toward which the light and sound in the image are subtly directed. Birds in the foreground are mid-flight but angled inward. A stream moves not toward the viewer but away, into the mass of the distance. A traveler at the very edge of the frame has stopped walking, not in fear but in the way a body stops when its spatial sense no longer works reliably. The god is the landscape. The landscape does not know the traveler is there. Deep green and grey watercolor, heavy ink linework for trees and stones, the distant center rendered in lighter, less-defined wash.

**What this captures:** The almost-mythic wild god — too old and large to perceive mortals, ambient rather than active, with navigation failure and sound absorption as effects.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (16:9)

### The Tragedy of Fragment 4 — The Absorbed

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of a small group of travelers in a wild landscape, moving deeper into a forest. They walk with the posture of people who believe they are approaching something — leaning forward, purposeful, expectant. But the forest around them is bending inward: trees angling toward a vanishing point ahead, sound visually implied as moving in one direction, the light contracting. The travelers' expressions are hopeful, intent, certain they have been received. The image does not show what is ahead. It only shows the direction of movement, and the landscape's patient, indifferent geometry. Fine ink on the travelers' faces; loose, absorbing watercolor for the forest closing in. Palette: deep green, shadow, a pale sky that is getting smaller.

**What this captures:** The tragedy of Fragment 4 — the group who interpreted the wild god's ambient presence as welcome and moved toward where the sound was going. The god did not refuse them. It simply does not know they are there.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (3:2)

## Readers of Decline

### The Network — Roads and Information

**Prompt:**
> An ink and watercolor illustration of a courier on horseback traveling along a road between two cities, at dusk. Saddlebags are full — grain contracts, correspondence, ordinary commerce. But in the corner of one saddlebag, barely visible, a small book with worn edges: a reader of decline's ledger, wrapped in merchant cloth. The road stretches ahead into the distance; other travelers are visible far behind. The courier herself is absorbed in the journey — not the information she carries, not its significance. The world around her is ordinary: a dust road, a cooling sky, a long ride ahead. Everything important is invisible. Fine ink on the courier's figure and the horse; loose watercolor for the road and sky. Palette: ochre and grey dusk, the road ordinary and significant at once.

**What this captures:** The structural connection between the commercial and reader-of-decline networks — the same roads carry grain contracts and the signals that route observers to sites of decline. The information that changes everything, moving unremarkably.

**Aspect ratio:** Landscape (3:2)
