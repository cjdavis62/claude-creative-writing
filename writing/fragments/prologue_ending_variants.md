# Ending Variants — *An Emergence*
*Three drafts of the revised ending, for author selection*
*Each picks up from: "The look on her face was not the look I knew for losing things."*

---

## VARIANT A — "What I Was"
*Focus: the god's gaze. He saw her and recognized something. She has spent her life not knowing what.*

---

The look on her face was not the look I knew for losing things.

Later, the village found words for what happened on the slope. They were all different words, and none of them were mine.

They had words for everything but the changes.

What I have never found a word for: that he saw me. Before I ran — while Margot stood still and the pines held and the air finished remembering how to be air — he looked across the slope and found me in it. His face changed. Not the way a face changes on a stranger. The way a face changes on someone who has been looking for a long time and has now found.

I have not stopped wondering what I was.

---

## VARIANT B — "The Small Bones"
*Focus: the physical mark she carries. The sensation of divine speech in her hands never quite left.*

---

The look on her face was not the look I knew for losing things.

Later, the village found words for what happened on the slope. They were all different words, and none of them were mine. They had words for everything but the changes.

I have one. It is not a word, but it is what I have.

The small bones of my hands. Where his word had been. In the years since, at certain moments — at thresholds, at the edges of things — I feel it again: the weight of something passing through on its way to somewhere else. I have crossed no more walls I was told not to cross.

But I know, now, the feeling of standing on the wrong side of one.

---

## VARIANT C — "The Third Wall"
*Focus: the witness's changed relationship to boundaries, crossings, the places that are not for us. Most cosmic in register.*

---

The look on her face was not the look I knew for losing things.

Later, the village found words for what happened on the slope. None of them were mine, and I did not offer them.

They had words for everything but the changes.

This is mine: the third wall has never looked the same to me since. It is only stones, stacked by someone long dead who did not want something to get in or out. But when I pass it now — and I have passed it many times, on errands, in my ordinary life — I feel the boundary of it differently than I did before. As if the line between *here* and *not for us* is a thing that can be seen, once you have crossed it at the wrong moment.

As if that line can also move.

---

## NOTES FOR AUTHOR

**Variant A** is the most character-focused and the most mysterious. "I have not stopped wondering what I was" — the tense (was, not am) implies that whatever she was to him may not be what she is now, or that she changed as a result of being seen. It plants a question the main narrative can answer, or refuse to answer. Most directly a cliffhanger in the traditional sense: a hook that pulls forward.

**Variant B** is the most bodily and the most thematically connected to the story's mechanics (the drain, the small bones as first signal, the sensitivity to threshold-crossings). "I know, now, the feeling of standing on the wrong side of one" implies she understands something about gateways that the village doesn't — setting up the larger story's concerns directly. More of a revelation than a cliffhanger.

**Variant C** is the most atmospheric and the most cosmologically resonant. "That line can also move" is the most ominous of the three endings — it implies the world's boundaries are unstable, which is exactly what the story is about. Most abstract; least personal. Strongest thematic hook; weakest character hook.

**Combinations worth considering:**
- A's final line + B's physical specificity: keep the bodily sensation of B but end on the wondering of A
- B's physical detail + C's final image: "I know the feeling of standing on the wrong side of one" is compatible with the small-bones setup
- Any variant can keep or drop the "They had words for everything but the changes" line — it functions as a hinge in all three
