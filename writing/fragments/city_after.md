The road in from the west had changed.

She noticed it before she could have named it — a settling in the ground to the south of the road, the field there lower than it should have been, its edge running at a different angle than she had known since childhood. In geological terms this was recent. In geological terms, recent meant within the last century.

She had been gone two years.

The city's smoke ahead was ordinary smoke. The smoke from two years ago had been something else. She had been three weeks east when she heard. She had kept going — the archive was expecting her, and the reports reached her by road, and she had read them in the careful language of people trying to describe something they did not have words for.

---

She came at the frozen district from the south end, where the boundary was clearest.

The city had put up rope and notices — the notices were supposed to keep people out, and instead gave the curious an accurate map of what not to miss. She stood at the rope.

The account she thought of most often was from a factor's clerk, a man whose uneven handwriting showed what the weeks after had done to him. *It did not come as noise. I have tried to explain this and people say this is wrong, that there was sound, and perhaps there was. But what I remember is a pressure. An understanding that the air outside had changed its nature. And then the way a door looks when it is about to open. And then — nothing moving. Everything staying exactly where it was.*

Everything staying exactly where it was.

That was what she was looking at.

A cart stood at the third corner, inside the boundary. The wood of the cart *is*, she corrected herself: present tense. Two years without weather. The window box on the building at the corner still held the plants from two years ago, stopped mid-season, the color unfaded.

---

The second account was from one of the preservation god's attendants. *We knew something had come. You know when a new presence enters the district — the marks in the doorframes catch the light differently. This was not that. This was a god that did not know the district already belonged to someone.* And then: *Our god began to extend the domain before I understood what it was extending it against. I felt it the way you feel a hand pressed flat on the back of a door. Whatever was on the other side was not the arriving god. The arriving god was also pushing that door.*

She had read this account thirty times. She read it differently here.

The arriving god had redirected the main flow. The rock and heat and gas that should have taken the central quarter: gone another way, at cost. The third account she had only read twice, because it was harder to hold. A woman who had been close enough to see the arriving god at the end: *Like it turned inside out. Like the inside of something becoming the outside of it.*

She had stopped there, both times. No available framework — and that itself was a kind of information.

---

She stood at the rope for a long time.

The stasis was still running.

She had known this — the reports noted it, the city administration noted it — but standing here, the knowing changed. Two years. The preservation god was in stasis, depleted past waking, and the domain was still operating.

The cart had not rotted.

She thought about the field to the south: the ground's recent settling, the edge of it running at an angle different from everything she had learned to call the shape of this land. She thought about what the texts said about the preservation god's domain in ordinary operation: *a gentle tendency toward the continuation of existing states*. Gentle. Ordinary. The kind of thing that read as stable geology until you knew to look for the pattern.

She thought about how long the god had been here — longer than the records.

She thought about what else, in this city and the land around it, had always seemed like simply the nature of things.

The afternoon was ordinary around the quiet of the frozen district. She did not write anything down.
