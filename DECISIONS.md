# Decisions

Append-only log of canon and process decisions, with the reasoning behind each. When a decision is revised, leave the old entry in place and add a new one referencing it. Entries are dated.

---

## 2026-05-01 — Working title: *The Doors of Gods*
**Decision:** *The Doors of Gods*.
**Reasoning:** Resonates with the gateway mechanic. "Doors" reads slightly more grammatically and tonally interesting than "Gates"; the plural "Gods" foregrounds plurality (council societies, the variety of divine presences). Held provisionally per `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §7; revisit after Phase 2.

## 2026-05-01 — Author byline: C. Davis and A. Claude
**Decision:** **C. Davis and A. Claude.**
**Reasoning:** Dual byline that is clear about the human/AI collaboration without belaboring it. Reads alphabetically, which gives the byline a natural rhythm.

## 2026-05-01 — First deliverable: fragment dossier
**Decision:** Four to six short fragments (500–1500 words each) set in this world but adjacent to the main plot, paired with a `QUESTIONS.md` log of questions arising during drafting.
**Reasoning:** Defers commitment to a longer form; exercises the agent pipeline on real prose; populates the wiki with concrete texture; surfaces voice questions in low-stakes contexts. See `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §3.4.

## 2026-05-01 — First fragment: an emergence witnessed by a child
**Decision:** Author's pick from the dossier candidates.
**Reasoning:** Engages the brainstorm's "founding moment before interpretation" theme (`brainstorm/god_society_dynamics_v3_1.md` §I); gives a specific concrete lens on the emergence mechanic; tight focal POV for testing voice in a low-overhead form.

## 2026-05-01 — Wiki structure
**Decision:** Top-level folders `wiki/characters/`, `wiki/locations/`, `wiki/concepts/`, `wiki/societies/`, `wiki/timeline/`. New folders added as the wiki demands them; the first creation of any new folder is announced rather than silent.
**Reasoning:** Per `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §7. Flat per-entity markdown is sufficient at this scale; the lore agents can grep across files without need for a vector store.

## 2026-05-01 — Pen name vs. real name
**Decision:** Pen name (C. Davis and A. Claude) rather than real name.
**Reasoning:** Author's preference per `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §7. Acknowledges the collaboration honestly without making it the entire identity of the work.

## 2026-05-01 — Hard boundary: no physical violence against children
**Decision:** The story does not depict physical violence against children. This is a hard boundary, only crossable if a scene *truly* requires it for irreplaceable narrative reasons (and even then it requires explicit author sign-off before drafting).
**Reasoning:** Author preference, raised in feedback on the v1 prologue draft (where a mother slapped the girl on her return from the emergence). The slap was meant to render the mother's terror; that work can be done other ways. This boundary should also be added to `STYLE_AND_VOICE.md` for the writer-agent's reference.

## 2026-05-01 — Prologue witness girl is not the protagonist
**Decision:** The unnamed girl in the prologue (`writing/fragments/prologue_emergence.md`) is *not* the protagonist as a child. She is a separate person who will recur later in the story, likely as an older woman — meaningful character or referenced figure, TBD.
**Reasoning:** Author decision per `QUESTIONS.md`. Implies a significant time gap between the prologue and the main story's "present." Frees the protagonist's gateway-marked backstory to be developed independently.

## 2026-05-01 — Prologue village is the protagonist's home village
**Decision:** The unnamed village in the prologue is the protagonist's home village. The god whose emergence is shown here is therefore part of the village's history that the protagonist will inherit (probably as a god long-since absent — fits the brainstorm's absent-god society model that shapes her).
**Reasoning:** Author decision per `QUESTIONS.md`. Gives the prologue structural weight: the village's defining theological event is the moment the reader meets it.

## 2026-05-01 — Identity of the god in the prologue (held open)
**Decision:** Deliberately not yet decided. Two paths to keep alive: (a) this is the dying deity of the main story arriving for his long mortal life, in which case the witness girl should haunt the narrative meaningfully; (b) this is a different god, in which case the prologue's job is to render the majesty/awe/weakness of arrival as a counterpoint to the present-day decline.
**Reasoning:** Author preference. Keeping the option open through the early fragments avoids prematurely committing.

## 2026-05-01 — Emergence is visibly a gateway
**Decision:** A god's emergence into the mortal world includes a visible "door" or upright threshold in the air, through which a glimpse of the divine plane (or somewhere not-here) can briefly be seen by witnesses. The door closes after the god comes through.
**Reasoning:** Established in the v2 prologue draft per author direction. Gives the emergence mechanic a concrete sensory referent in the prose; supports the project's working title (*The Doors of Gods*); fits the brainstorm's "physical signature" of emergence (`brainstorm/god_society_dynamics_v3_1.md` §I) without contradicting prior canon.

## 2026-05-01 — Divine speech is felt bodily
**Decision:** Words spoken by gods (especially newly-emerged ones) are perceived bodily as well as audibly by mortal witnesses — felt in the bones, the chest, the small joints. Not all the time; the prose can render this when it serves the moment.
**Reasoning:** Established in the v2 prologue draft. Marks divine speech as physically distinct without requiring constant explanation. Also a subtle hook for the gateway-mark mechanic (gateway-marked mortals may eventually be shown to perceive this differently).

## 2026-05-01 — Pre-emergence effects: animals sense the membrane straining
**Decision:** Before a god's actual emergence, the membrane between planes is straining at the site for some duration (hours, probably). Humans do not typically perceive this; animals do. The effects: domestic animals across the surrounding area become restless and refuse normal behaviors; animals in close proximity to the site are drawn toward it and walk there with uncharacteristic directness, ignoring grass and other distractions; animals that have arrived first stand still, attentive to a point in the air where there is nothing yet. The implication: the actual *first* witness of any emergence is almost always an animal, and the first human witness is whoever happened to follow the animal.
**Reasoning:** Established in the v3 prologue draft per author direction. Resolves the brainstorm's note that "emergences are sudden" with the desire for the emergence to have signs that connect to the goat: the suddenness is at the level of human perception, not at the level of physical mechanism. This also gives us a recurring world-texture motif (animals as the unread first signs) and reframes the brainstorm's "first witness mythology" — the entity that actually saw it first is rarely human and is never given authority. Logged in `wiki/concepts/emergence.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — Pre-emergence effects: celestial signs
**Decision:** Emergence also disturbs the night sky in subtle ways perceptible to attentive sky-watchers and those who track the seasonal sky as part of their work. Specifically: known constellations show wrong angles or extra stars (one star too bright for the season, one star slightly displaced, stars showing in places between the known constellations); the night sky as a whole feels too bright and too sharp, the way a too-clear winter night feels even out of season. The wrongness is never so gross as a rearrangement of stars — only ever the kind of wrongness a child knows in her bones but cannot defend if asked.
**Reasoning:** Established in the v4 prologue per author direction. Author rejected an earlier proposal (a named "wandering star" called the Walker) as too on-the-nose. The subtler version preserves the unread-sign motif and gives sky-knowledge to certain figures (herders, sky-watchers) without making the celestial sign legible to the village at large. Logged in `wiki/concepts/emergence.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — Pre-emergence sensitivity: thresholds of life
**Decision:** Heightened sensitivity to emergence extends from animals to humans at the *thresholds of life* — the very young and the very old. Children sometimes witness emergences the adults around them do not perceive; the very old are similarly drawn or unsettled. The pairing of young and old around an emergence is itself a sign, though rarely read as one until the mythology has had time to form. This is one reason emergences are so often witnessed by figures who would not otherwise have been present: a child sent to fetch a goat; an elderly herder; a dying scholar.
**Reasoning:** Author canon addition during v4 plan review. Beautifully extends the brainstorm's note that "the person who actually witnessed the emergence first is not always the person who ends up with the authority." Witnesses are at the edges of human cognition because the membrane is more easily felt there. Logged in `wiki/concepts/emergence.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — Margot is a named character (the witness's family goat)
**Decision:** **Old Margot** is the witness girl's family goat in the prologue. Old. Has scratched her right horn off; scratches her left horn against a wall. Has a place she lies down at midday with her old eyes half-closed. Eats first, eats even when there are wolves, does not lift her head for anything — until the night of the emergence. After the emergence she is changed: she comes down from the slope four days later in pa's company and never grazes the lower terraces again.
**Reasoning:** Author's introduction during the v3 edit. Per `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §4.4 every character — including animals — has agency. Margot is one of the unmythologized changed beings the prologue's closing names: the village will have words for the man on the slope but no words for what changed in Margot. Logged in `wiki/characters/margot.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — Pa is a named family member (offstage in prologue, brings the god down later)
**Decision:** **Pa** is the witness's father. A herder; manages the family's herd; was at the high pasture during the prologue, two days from home. Wry. Gallows humor. Has been close enough to wolves to "rush the herd away" once. Has a saying about Margot the witness was not supposed to know. Four days after the night of the emergence, pa returns from the high pasture in the company of the man who emerged on the slope — and with Margot. He will not say where he picked up the company. The village's eventual mythology of the emergence is built around the moment pa walks down with the god. The actual moment on the slope, witnessed by the girl, is never given canonical place in the mythology.
**Reasoning:** Author addition during v4 plan review. Keeps pa offstage in the prologue (cleaner cast, ma onstage for the return) while planting the seed that the village's first contact with the god is via pa. Lets the mythology form around pa-and-the-stranger and not around the girl's actual witness, reinforcing the closing's "they had words for everything but the changes." Logged in `wiki/characters/pa.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — The witness is changed by what she saw, but does not know it
**Decision:** The witness girl returns from the slope changed — possibly gateway-marked, possibly something else. She does not know it. She thinks the looks she gets at home (and later, from the village) are about the missing goat and her late return. The closing of the prologue plants this without naming it: ma's face when she sees her daughter is "not the look I knew for losing things."
**Reasoning:** Author direction during v4 plan review. Establishes the emergence as transformative for the witnesses (Margot AND the girl), not only for the god. The unmythologized changes are the ones the prologue is most about. The closing line — "they had words for everything but the changes" — names this thematically. Reinforces `PROJECT_PLAN.md` §4.4 commitment to character agency: the witness's interiority is real even when the village's mythology displaces her.

## 2026-05-01 — POV shift: the prologue is in first person
**Decision:** The prologue is narrated in first person by the witness girl, looking back from later in life. The rest of the novel (Thread A and Thread B) remains close third per `STYLE_AND_VOICE.md`. The prologue's first-person framing is a deliberate seam: the prologue is *a remembered account*; the rest of the novel is third-person live time.
**Reasoning:** Author preference per v4 plan. The reader meets the world through the child's eyes; the child meets it for the first time herself; the doubled newness is part of the prologue's work. The framing also gives the closing "they had words for everything but the changes" greater weight — the narrator is one of those who was changed and never named.

## 2026-05-01 — Voice register established (project-wide)
**Decision:** Voice exemplar paragraph (the user's edit at line 52 of the v3 prologue) is the project's prose anchor. Body-first, named specifics, mixed tenses for subjective time, reported speech retold in child grammar, parenthetical asides in child voice, stakes carried in vocabulary. Banned: any narrator-commentary on the *mode* of perception ("the way a child notices…", "she knew the pattern of it…"). Logged in `STYLE_AND_VOICE.md` (Voice Exemplars + Anti-patterns sections) and `skills/fantasy-writer/SKILL.md` (Interiority through action and observation + Telling that masquerades as showing).
**Reasoning:** v3 of the prologue was diagnosed as "competent literary prose, not alive prose" — leaning on generic-narrator framing that sounds literary but does no work. v4 commits to a higher bar across all future drafts.

## 2026-05-01 — New skill: creative-writing-instructor
**Decision:** Add `skills/creative-writing-instructor/SKILL.md`, a professor-level craft skill that handles questions about sentence rhythm, POV, voice, dialogue, scene structure, pacing, and prose diagnosis. The fantasy-writer skill remains the principles reference; the creative-writing-instructor is the deeper teaching that lets those principles operate at high specificity. Use it when the question is *"is this prose any good, and why or why not, and what would make it better?"*
**Reasoning:** Author direction during v4 prologue review. The I-monotony in v4 (too many sentences starting with *I* in first-person prose) was a craft-level issue that wasn't covered crisply in any existing skill. The new skill includes specific guidance on sentence-opener variety (first-person prose targets 25–35% I-starts, not 60–70%), among other professional-level craft tools.

## 2026-05-01 — Prologue framing: Ma sends the witness, witness asks for relief
**Decision:** In the prologue's opening, ma sends the witness girl out to find Margot (the goat is missing from her pen). The girl asks twice if she has to be the one to look, and ma says yes both times; the third time the girl asks, ma does not answer at all and turns her face away. So the girl goes. This clarifies the dynamic: ma is the one who decides; the girl is reluctant; ma's silence is harder than her refusal would have been.
**Reasoning:** Author edit/comment during v4 review — the original framing ("I asked Ma twice and she said no twice") read as ambiguous about what was being asked. The clearer dynamic (ma sends, girl asks for relief, ma's silence settles it) is more emotionally legible.

## 2026-05-01 — Village texture: the autumn peddler
**Decision:** The protagonist's home village is visited by a peddler in autumn — a regular seasonal arrival, bringing strange cloth from somewhere with a name the witness girl cannot say. The peddler is a known reference point for "outsider" or "from elsewhere" in the village's frame.
**Reasoning:** Established in v5 prologue as the child's reference point for the god's clothes — she tries to fit the man on the slope into categories she has, and the peddler is the closest available. The fit fails. The child cannot place him. Logged because future fragments may reuse the peddler as a way to render the village's relationship to the wider world.

## 2026-05-01 — Pa took the witness stargazing one winter night
**Decision:** Pa took his daughter out to look at the stars one winter night and went still when he thought he heard wolves. This is part of the witness's family memory of pa — quiet, watchful, willing to teach her the constellations on what became her favorite nights.
**Reasoning:** Author edit during v4 review. Adds texture to pa as a teacher (he is the source of her star-knowledge, which matters for the prologue's "the constellations are wrong" beat). Also sources the way Margot's stillness is described in the prose: *Still the way pa was still when he thought he heard wolves last winter while taking me out to look at the stars.*
