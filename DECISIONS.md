## 2026-05-27 — The protagonist's backstory wound: the twin and the village god

**Decision:** The protagonist's specific backstory wound involves a twin whose bond with the protagonist was cosmologically deepened by the ambient domain of a village patron god — a different god from the dying deity of the main story. The village god had a domain involving the coherence of bonds between people (precise formulation TBD). Its ambient presence deepened the twin bond into something the world recognized: each twin perceived more fully through the other's existence. The gift was real.

The village god declined. As its domain failed, the deepened bond became unstable. The break was asymmetric. The specific nature of the wound: the protagonist is now dead to the twin — the twin can no longer experience the protagonist as real. Not through will or rejection, but through the mechanism of the bond's dissolution. The twin is alive, is kind, completes their shared phrases from muscle memory. There is no felt response. The protagonist is the ghost. She mourns for both of them because the twin cannot mourn.

She still visits. The worst visit — the time she thought it was working — exists in her body. She does not return to it directly.

**Reasoning:** Author decision, worldbuilding session 2026-05-27. Selected from among several candidate wounds and fate options. This wound was chosen because: (1) it is grounded in the world's specific mechanics (ambient domain, decline, bond as cosmologically real); (2) it produces a worldview earned through loss rather than ideology; (3) the "present but not landing" grief maps to universally recognizable human experiences (unreachable loved ones, asymmetric loss) without requiring readers to share the fantastical specifics; (4) the wound's structure — gift real, taking ongoing and invisible until catastrophic — rhymes precisely with the story's central irony about the imitation gateways. The village god is explicitly a different god from the dying deity; her hatred at the tree is not personal to him but aimed at the apparatus he represents.

## 2026-05-27 — The protagonist's worldview: gods as takers; her as microcosm of world's disillusionment

**Decision:** The protagonist's defining worldview is not that gods never gave — she knows the gift was real — but that the giving doesn't balance the taking, and most people can't see the ledger until it's too late. She arrived at this through personal loss rather than ideology. This positions her as a microcosm of what the world is now experiencing at civilizational scale: the same structure (gift real, devotion continuing, taking ongoing after the giving has withdrawn) playing out in her personal history before it played out everywhere.

Her relationship to the world's other responses to decline is as follows: she recognizes people in intensified piety as paying a cost she knows; she finds the anti-deity movements recognizable but wrong in their dismissal of the gift's reality; she is closest in position to the readers of decline; she recognizes the flatness of communities that have survived a maddened god's aftermath.

**Reasoning:** Author direction, worldbuilding session 2026-05-27. The twin wound alone makes her sympathetic; this layer makes her structurally significant to the novel's argument. Her position — unable to dismiss the gift, unable to forgive the taking — is the story's most honest accounting of what the decline actually costs. It also sets up the anti-deity movements as a distorted mirror: they've arrived at a similar conclusion through reasoning rather than loss and have made it manageable in ways she hasn't, which she finds less impressive than straightforward opposition.

## 2026-05-16 — Threshold-of-life sensitivity extends to decline as well as emergence

**Decision:** The same underlying principle that makes the very young and very old more sensitive to divine emergence also applies to divine decline. A declining god's residual presence operates at a finer, more idealistic frequency than practical adult perception easily registers. The young and the elderly are more open to this register — not because they are specially gifted, but because adult life has not yet (or has no longer) trained them to discount what they feel in favor of the practical and realistic. Adults who have attended years of ritual have implicitly calibrated against the world-as-it-is; a fraying domain cannot easily reach through that accommodation. The young and old haven't built it. This is why the witness-god in Solaven responded to a twelve-year-old in Fragment 3 when it failed to respond to forty adults: not because it chose her, but because she was still receivable.

**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-16, confirmed at Phase 2 review. Closes QUESTIONS.md item 2026-05-09. Consistent with the brainstorm's threshold-of-life canon from the prologue; extends it from emergence sensitivity to decline sensitivity through the same underlying logic. The practical implication: in any scene involving a declining god's ritual or presence, children and the elderly are the diagnostic group — if anyone is still receiving something, it will be them.

## 2026-05-16 — The declining god in Vanaek: domain of temporal coordination

**Decision:** The declining god in Vanaek has a domain of **temporal coordination** — not time itself (too vast for a single god's domain), but the felt sense that things happen when they should: the bell rings at the right moment, the meeting convenes when it ought to, the rhythms of daily communal life stay in phase with each other. Its failure manifests as timing slippage throughout Vanaek's daily rhythms — late bells, meetings that drift, the sense that the city's pulse is losing its beat. This is the specific component of the accord domain that Fragment 2 was already rendering through the late bell and the mis-rhythms: temporal coordination was always the sub-domain making the scene legible as decline. The accord domain of Fragment 2's broader description now names one of its constituent elements more precisely.

**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-16, confirmed at Phase 2 review. Closes QUESTIONS.md item 2026-05-05. The late bells in Fragment 2 were already planting this without naming it — the fragment is now retroactively more precisely grounded. Future scenes in Vanaek or referencing Vanaek should treat timing failure as the domain-specific signature: things being late, out of phase, arriving at the wrong moment. The god's full identity and name remain withheld.

## 2026-05-16 — Conscious vs. subconscious divine power

**Decision:** Gods have both conscious domain expression and subconscious/ambient domain effects that operate without the god's active awareness. These are distinct. A god consciously using its domain is a deliberate act — it knows what it is doing and why. An ambient domain effect is the passive consequence of a god's nature being present in a place: the world around the god conforms to the shape of its domain without the god directing it to do so. The god may not know this is happening. The preservation god in Fragment 5 held back the volcanic forces beneath the city as an ambient effect of its stasis domain — not a deliberate choice, not a known act, simply what happened in the vicinity of a god whose nature is the continuation of existing states. When the incoming god's presence awoke those forces, the preservation god had no prepared response because it had not known there was anything to respond to.

**Implications:**
- A declining god may be unknowingly failing to maintain things nobody has attributed to divine intervention. The failure becomes visible as a "natural" change — geological, ecological, social — that observers have no framework to connect to a god.
- What mortals call stable geology, reliable weather, healthy ecosystems, or functioning communities may include subconscious divine maintenance that has become indistinguishable from nature because it has always been there.
- This is a different kind of tragedy than visible decline: not what is lost consciously, but what silently fails. The god does not know it is failing to hold something. The mortals do not know there was ever a god holding it.
- Future fragments and the main narrative should distinguish carefully between a god's deliberate domain use and its ambient effects. The two can be dramatically opposed: a god who cannot consciously use its domain at full strength may still be producing powerful ambient effects.

**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-16, arising from Fragment 5 (The City After). The key discovery of that fragment — that the stasis domain is still running even though the god is depleted — implies a domain that operates below the level of consciousness. The writing group session noted that this reframes divine decline: not gods becoming less, but the consequences of their ambient absence becoming newly legible. This decision formalizes that mechanic as a world-building principle.

## 2026-05-01 — Pre-emergence effects: animals sense the membrane straining
**Decision:** Before a god's actual emergence, the membrane between planes is straining at the site for some duration (hours, probably). Humans do not typically perceive this; animals do. The effects: domestic animals across the surrounding area become restless and refuse normal behaviors; animals in close proximity to the site are drawn toward it and walk there with uncharacteristic directness, ignoring grass and other distractions; animals that have arrived first stand still, attentive to a point in the air where there is nothing yet. The implication: the actual *first* witness of any emergence is almost always an animal, and the first human witness is whoever happened to follow the animal.
**Reasoning:** Established in the v3 prologue draft per author direction. Resolves the brainstorm's note that "emergences are sudden" with the desire for the emergence to have signs that connect to the goat: the suddenness is at the level of human perception, not at the level of physical mechanism. This also gives us a recurring world-texture motif (animals as the unread first signs) and reframes the brainstorm's "first witness mythology" — the entity that actually saw it first is rarely human and is never given authority. Logged in `wiki/concepts/emergence.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — Pre-emergence effects: celestial signs
**Decision:** Emergence also disturbs the night sky in subtle ways perceptible to attentive sky-watchers and those who track the seasonal sky as part of their work. Specifically: known constellations show wrong angles or extra stars (one star too bright for the season, one star slightly displaced, stars showing in places between the known constellations); the night sky as a whole feels too bright and too sharp, the way a too-clear winter night feels even out of season. The wrongness is never so gross as a rearrangement of stars — only ever the kind of wrongness a child knows in her bones but cannot defend if asked.
**Reasoning:** Established in the v4 prologue per author direction. Author rejected an earlier proposal (a named "wandering star" called the Walker) as too on-the-nose. The subtler version preserves the unread-sign motif and gives sky-knowledge to certain figures (herders, sky-watchers) without making the celestial sign legible to the village at large. Logged in `wiki/concepts/emergence.md`.

## 2026-05-01 — Pre-emergence sensitivity: thresholds of life
**Decision:** Heightened sensitivity to emergence extends from animals to humans at the *thresholds of life* — the very young and the very old. Children sometimes witness emergences the adults around them do not perceive; the very old are similarly drawn or unsettled. The pairing of young and old around an emergence is itself a sign, though rarely read as one until the mythology has had time to form. This is one reason emergences are so often witnessed by figures who would not otherwise have been present: a child sent to fetch a goat; an elderly herder; a dying scholar.
**Reasoning:** Author canon addition during v4 plan review. Witnesses are at the edges of human cognition because the membrane is more easily felt there. Logged in `wiki/concepts/emergence.md`.

## 2026-05-09 — Naming framework: patron-model society (provisional)

**Decision:** The patron-model society in Fragment 3 uses a distinct linguistic character from Vanaek's council culture. Where Vanaek is precise and clipped (short vowels, moderate consonants), this culture is more flowing and oral: open vowels (a, o, u prominent), soft consonants (l, m, n, r, v), longer personal names (2–3 syllables common), diphthongs (-ao, -ua, -ol, -ael). Place names tend toward single long words or descriptive compounds encoding the community's relationship to the god. Settlement name: **Solaven** (sol = kept/held; -aven = place of — "the kept place," fitting a society organized around continuity and memory). Clergy names: formal but flowing, ending in -ael or -oen. All names provisional pending Phase 5 linguist review.
**Reasoning:** Author direction that different regions should have genuinely distinct cultures and languages (DECISIONS.md 2026-05-05). The oral/memory register fits a society whose god's domain involves continuity and witness. The contrast with Vanaek's clipped precision should be legible even in the names.

## 2026-05-09 — The god in Solaven has a domain of witness/continuity

**Decision:** The patron god of Solaven has a domain of **witness** — specifically the quality of having one's life acknowledged, the chain of continuity that makes individual existence feel held within something larger. The ritual practice: community members come before the god's presence to speak something — a loss, an accomplishment, a name — and have it received. When the god's presence is full, this exchange is felt as real. When declining, people go through the form and nothing answers. The reader in Fragment 3 is watching for the failure of that exchange: the moment when someone speaks and the quality of the room doesn't change.
**Reasoning:** Abstract domain, per the 2026-05-05 decision that not all gods need elemental/traditional domains. Witness/continuity fits the patron-model's intimacy (a single god deeply embedded in daily life) and produces observable decline signs that are social and qualitative — difficult to name, easy to feel. Also thematically resonant with the project's central concern: the gods' leaving means no one witnesses, nothing is held.

## 2026-05-05 — Gods need not have elemental or traditional domains

**Decision:** Not all gods in this world have the elemental/nature domains familiar from Greco-Roman mythology (harvest, sea, war, fire, etc.). Some gods — particularly those associated with council societies and more philosophically sophisticated cultures — have significantly more abstract domains: things like accord, memory, the quality of skilled attention, the coherence of a community's sense of itself. These abstract domains are no less real and no less powerful than elemental ones; they are simply harder to name, which makes their failure harder to notice and harder to explain.
**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-05. Prevents the world from defaulting to a generic fantasy-mythology palette. Abstract domains also fit the world's central irony more tightly: a god whose domain is something invisible and social will produce a decline that is felt as ambient wrongness rather than concrete failure — exactly the texture the world needs.

## 2026-05-05 — The declining god in Vanaek has a domain of accord

**Decision:** One of Vanaek's council gods has a domain of **accord** — not harmony in a soft or emotional sense, but the invisible coordination substrate of human community: the quality that makes a room of people able to share space without friction, that lets skilled collaborators work without talking through every step, that makes conversation flow because each person naturally finds the pause meant for them. When present and functioning, it is unnoticeable; it is simply how being with other people feels. Its failure is felt as a diffuse social wrongness — mis-rhythms, small coordination failures, the accumulating sense that the room keeps not quite working — before anyone has language for it.
**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-05. Chosen over elemental/agricultural options specifically because it is abstract and non-traditional. Also has broader-world implications: the decline of accord-gods across multiple societies is one of the hidden drivers of the world becoming "more insular, more violent" (per `brainstorm/story_outline_v2.md`). Communities losing this domain attribute the resulting friction to everything except what's actually happening. The Fragment 2 revision reflects this: the mis-rhythms are social and conversational, not environmental or physical.

## 2026-05-05 — Fragment 2 revision: diffuse mis-rhythms replace the late bell

**Decision:** The late bell in the first draft of *The Vaelen at the Third Bell* is too legible as a sign — most laypeople would not track bell timing closely enough for a late bell to register as specifically wrong. Replace with accumulated diffuse mis-rhythms that are downstream of the accord domain failing: two people reaching for the same thing simultaneously and neither yielding, a joke landing flat, someone losing the thread of a sentence they've told a hundred times, the server refilling the wrong cup, Relen and Sorin's own shorthand requiring a beat more effort than usual. Sorin's tell also changes: instead of a prepared non-reaction to a specific event, Sorin is *too deliberate* — holding their own coordination manually, still and precise in a room that is quietly losing its beat. Revises the fragment to second draft.
**Reasoning:** Author note 2026-05-05. The first draft turned on a sign (legible, pointable); the second draft turns on texture (felt before named). This is a more honest rendering of how the accord domain's failure would actually manifest, and fits the world's broader pattern of decline-as-unread-texture.

## 2026-05-05 — Naming framework: council-society culture (provisional)

**Decision:** The council-society culture (site of the Tavern fragment) uses the following provisional framework: short clean vowels (a, e, i, o), occasional diphthongs (-ae, -oi, -en), moderate consonants (b, d, v, k, t, s, f, m, n) — no heavy clusters, no rolled r, no gutturals. ## 2026-05-05 — World-building principle: cultural and linguistic distinctiveness across regions

**Decision:** Different regions of the world have genuinely distinct cultures, histories, perspectives, and languages — including distinct naming conventions. The council-society framework established above applies to that culture only. Future fragments set in other society types (patron-model, absent-god, rejection-model, wild margins) should establish their own linguistic character before drafting, drawing on the specific history, geography, and god-relationship of that culture. Intermixing between cultures is real and should be reflected in multilingual characters, borrowed vocabulary, and the texture of contact-zones.
**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-05. The world's strength is its tight causal logic (gateway mechanic → decline → social response) applied differently in different places. Linguistic distinctiveness is one of the most efficient ways to make locations feel genuinely different rather than variations on a single generic fantasy-medieval palette. The protagonist's journey through multiple society types should feel like moving through genuinely different worlds.

## 2026-05-05 — Gods and gender expression: non-normative is the norm; clergy mirrors it

**Decision:** Gods in this world tend toward gender expression that is non-normative, fluid, or outside mortal categories — this is a feature of divine nature rather than an exception. Some humans in clerical and god-adjacent roles (sign-watchers, naming-ceremony practitioners, close attendants) mirror this, either through conscious religious identification or through the long-term effect of proximity to divine presence. This does not require explicit in-text commentary — it is woven into how these characters are described and named. It is not the character's plot function; it is part of who they are.
**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-05 (Phase 2 check-in). Extends the existing §4.4 commitment and the STYLE_AND_VOICE.md note that "gods may have varying or non-binary gender presentation." The author's specific insight — that clergy *mirror* divine gender expression — makes this a cosmological logic rather than just an inclusivity commitment: proximity to the divine plane does something to how people experience and express gender. This is most visible in clergy, but not exclusive to them. The naming framework for the council society should reflect this: clergy names should not carry the same gender-marking as lay names.

## 2026-05-10 — Emergence witnesses can be physically altered by proximity to a crossing

**Decision:** Close witnesses to a god's emergence may be physically changed in ways that echo the nature of the crossing itself. The change is permanent, not always immediately noticed, and never explained by the village or the witness. The specific alteration varies by the character of the emergence. For the prologue witness: the emergence involved inversion (the air and ground flipped, the light was below her feet) — and her handedness flipped with it. She went up the slope right-handed and came down left-handed. She does not name this. The prose plants it through action and names it plainly once in the ending without explanation.

**Reasoning:** Author direction 2026-05-10. The handedness flip is established in `writing/fragments/prologue_emergence.md` (v6): the witness uses her right hand on the stone going up; the rope is in her left hand when she moves toward Margot just before running; the ending states "I came up that slope with my right hand on the stone. I did not come down with it." The change is consistent with the world's existing mechanic — mortals near imitation gateways are drained and narrowed; a mortal at the centre of an actual emergence is inverted by it, which is a different and more total kind of alteration. This also distinguishes the witness's change from the gateway-mark (which is a slow drain) and from Margot's change (behavioral, not physical). Future instances of this mechanic should vary by the character of the specific god/emergence rather than defaulting to handedness. Canon question left open: does the physical alteration compound with the metaphysical (the changed threshold-sensitivity established in the ending)? Likely yes, but not yet settled.
